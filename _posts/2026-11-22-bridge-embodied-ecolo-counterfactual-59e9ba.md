## 1. Counterexamples at the Causality and Epistemology Boundary

Mapping where each standalone formulation degrades sets the scope cleanly. Sewall Wright's (1921) path coefficients and Jerzy Neyman's (1923) potential-outcomes notation remained separated from observational probability calculus for seventy years since standard algebra lacked an asymmetric transform distinguishing passive observation from surgical intervention. Donald Rubin (1974) formalized counterfactual assignment couplings in clinical trials, and Judea Pearl (1995, 2009) unified formal equations with graphical d-separation through the three-rule `do`-calculus, characterizing full graphical constraints for non-parametric causal identifiability (Pearl, 2009). Decoupling Causality from Epistemology overlooks how local variance compounds across partner state variables.

Genuine epistemic competence confirms itself less in swift answers on familiar ground than in calibrated restraint when available support underdetermines the conclusion (Guo et al., 2017). Modern neural dependability audits by Chuan Guo et al. (2017) revealed that increasing graph depth and cross-entropy over-training enhances top-1 classification precision while driving Expected Alignment Residual upward by $3\times$ to $5\times$ through logit norm inflation. Post-hoc temperature scaling on a held-out validation split restores alignment slope without altering rank fidelity, whereas profile shift requires ensemble dispersion or conformal risk control (Gneiting & Raftery, 2007).

## 2. Joint Operator Structure: Graph Surgery and Barometric Symmetry and Proper Scoring Rules and Empirical Hit Rates

Formulating the Causality × Epistemology junction directly on partner coordinates (Out-of-Support Inferential Tail Fraction) links Back-Door P(Y|do(X)) to Calibrated Abstention (Guo et al., 2017).

$$ P(Y \mid \mathrm{do}(X = x)) = \sum_{z} P(Y \mid X = x, Z = z)\, P(Z = z) $$

**Explicit Non-Equivalence Boundary (Causality vs. Epistemology).** Structural causal identification ($P(Y \mid \mathrm{do}(X))$ via graph surgery) is a property of the causal DAG, whereas proper scoring calibration (Brier or logarithmic risk) is an observational distribution property (Pearl, 2009). Zero observational calibration error never implies unbiased causal estimation under unmeasured confounding (Gneiting & Raftery, 2007).

**Formal Coupling via Back-Door Stratification Under Measurement Compression.** Connecting Graph Surgery and Barometric Symmetry and Proper Scoring Rules and Measured Hit Rates rigorously requires evaluating how coarse-graining an adjustment proxy $Z_b = (1 - c(b))U + (\sigma_z / b)\varepsilon_Z$ ($b = e^{\eta_{AB}}$) alters both the back-door estimator and post-adjustment predictive risk:

$$ \widehat{\mathrm{ATE}} _ {\mathrm{do}}(b) = \sum_{k=1}^{8} \frac{n_k}{N}\left(\bar{Y} _ {X=1,k} - \bar{Y} _ {X=0,k}\right), \qquad \mathrm{Brier}(b) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(Y_i - \hat{p}_i(X_i, Z_{b,i})\right)^2 $$
