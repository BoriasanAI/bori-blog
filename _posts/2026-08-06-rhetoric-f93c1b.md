# Toward a Definition of Rhetoric

## Abstract

This paper examines rhetoric as a structured concept rather than a primitive. Taking its standard definition as a point of departure, it isolates the properties that give the concept its internal organization and argues that these properties, not the label, are what the concept's explanatory work depends on.

## 1. Introduction

The concept of rhetoric is widely invoked yet unevenly specified. A working definition holds that rhetoric is the art of persuasion. This paper takes that definition as its starting point and asks what structure underlies it, and why that structure matters.

## 2. Structure and Properties

A defining property is the following: It is one of the three ancient arts of discourse (trivium) of classical antiquity, along with grammar and logic/dialectic. This property is not incidental; it constrains how rhetoric may be instantiated and marks it off from adjacent concepts with which it is frequently conflated.

## 3. Conclusion

This analysis has treated rhetoric not as a self-evident given but as a structured concept whose properties can be stated and examined. The organization surveyed above suggests that its apparent simplicity conceals a definite internal structure, one that rewards further and more formal study.
