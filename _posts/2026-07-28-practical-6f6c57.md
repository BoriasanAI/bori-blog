# Rethinking Practical

It is tempting to treat practical as self-evident, yet the moment one looks closely the idea resists a simple answer. At its core, practical is a philosophical tradition that views language and thought as tools for prediction, problem solving, and action, rather than describing, representing, or mirroring reality. That is a starting point, not a conclusion, and the distance between the two is what these paragraphs try to trace.

None of this settles the question of practical for good. What it suggests is that the concept is sturdier than its casual use implies, and that beneath the shorthand lies a structure worth taking seriously. The ideas we treat as obvious are usually the ones most worth re-examining.
