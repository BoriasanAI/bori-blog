# Universals: Structure and Consequence

Few ideas are invoked as often, and examined as rarely, as universals. The usual shorthand, that universals is In metaphysics, a universal is what particular things have in common, namely characteristics or qualities, is accurate enough, yet it leaves the more interesting question untouched: why does the concept hold together at all?

The concept does not stand alone; it borders on There, a neighbouring idea whose boundary helps mark its own, and the two are best understood together rather than apart.

To call universals well understood would be premature; to call it mysterious would be lazy. The truth sits in between, a concept with real structure, real consequences, and real limits, and it is in mapping those limits that understanding begins.
