# On the Nature of Four

Few ideas are invoked as often, and examined as rarely, as four. The usual shorthand, that four is a number, numeral and digit, is accurate enough, yet it leaves the more interesting question untouched: why does the concept hold together at all?

What complicates the picture is this: it is the natural number following 3 and preceding 5. The idea is less tidy than its textbook form suggests, and that untidiness is precisely where its interest lies.

A further point follows from the first. It is a square number, the smallest semiprime and composite number, and is considered unlucky in many East Asian cultures, and once that is granted, several apparent puzzles resolve into ordinary consequences of the same structure.

To call four well understood would be premature; to call it mysterious would be lazy. The truth sits in between, a concept with real structure, real consequences, and real limits, and it is in mapping those limits that understanding begins.
