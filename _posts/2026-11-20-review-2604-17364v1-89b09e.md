## Abstract

In *LLM-Guided Strategy Synthesis for Scalable Equality Saturation* (`arXiv:2604.17364v1`), Chenyun Yin, Youwei Xiao, et al. (4 authors) test how `cs.AI` architectural choices behave under realistic resource and distribution-shift constraints. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17364v1#abstract" aria-label="Source 1: Abstract">[1]</a></sup>

Equality saturation (EqSat) is a powerful optimization paradigm that compactly represents many equivalent programs in an e-graph and delays commitment until extraction selects a lowest-cost program. The search lacks reusable strategy abstractions and actionable feedback, and can easily trigger e-graph explosion or converge to poor designs.

Checked against controlled baselines, the empirical findings isolate which design stages carry genuine load under stress.

## 1. Motivation and Scope: Introduction

Equality saturation (EqSat) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for program optimization by exploring vast spaces of equivalent programs without premature commitment to specific rewrite sequences. By employing an e-graph to represent an exponential number of variations compactly, EqSat decouples the exploration of equivalent forms from the final extraction based on a cost model. This separation has yielded state-of-the-art results in electronic design automation, program synthesis, and compiler optimization. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17364v1#S1" aria-label="Source 2: 1. Introduction">[2]</a></sup>

Mitigating this explosion necessitates a focus on strategy. In the manuscript, a strategy is a control structure for organizing search, including partitioning rulesets, scheduling saturation, simplifying e-graph states, and budget concerns. Strategy-guided EqSat bridges the gap between exhaustive exploration and rigid rewrites. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17364v1#S1" aria-label="Source 3: 1. Introduction">[3]</a></sup>

Despite its importance, manual strategy design remains the primary obstacle to achieving full automation in e-graph-based compilers. Although rule synthesis has been successfully automated by frameworks like Enumo and Ruler, these tools address only one aspect of the automation puzzle. Crucially, the resulting influx of rules paradoxically exacerbates e-graph explosion by expanding the search branching factor. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17364v1#S1" aria-label="Source 4: 1. Introduction">[4]</a></sup>

## 2. Conceptual Architecture: Background and Motivation

Precision across `2604.17364v1` begins with Chenyun Yin, Youwei Xiao, et al. (4 authors)'s explicit partitioning of terms and state boundaries:

This section reviews the background of equality saturation and strategy-guided search, situates our work within the landscape of existing EqSat strategy approaches, and motivates EggMind by identifying the limitations of direct code evolution. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17364v1#S2" aria-label="Source 5: 2. Background and Motivation">[5]</a></sup>

Equality saturation (EqSat) utilizes e-graphs to compactly represent equivalent programs while deferring commitment until extraction selects a lowest-cost program, and is implemented in systems such as egg and egglog. The e-graph is organized into e-classes, which group equivalent e-nodes. Each e-node represents functional operators with other e-classes as their children. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17364v1#S2" aria-label="Source 6: 2. Background and Motivation">[6]</a></sup>

In practice, exposing all rewrites at once can trigger rapid e-graph growth and quickly make unguided EqSat intractable under realistic resource budgets. To address this problem, a strategy organizes the search space without altering rewrite semantics. It partitions rules into sequenced phases, inserts simplification points, and assigns execution budgets. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17364v1#S2" aria-label="Source 7: 2. Background and Motivation">[7]</a></sup>

Expressing the bounded-resource optimization frontier, with $R_{\mathrm{proxy}}(\pi_k)$ denoting training proxy return, $R_{\mathrm{true}}(\pi_k)$ denoting out-of-sample fidelity, and $N_c$ denoting stage capacity:

$$\begin{aligned} R_{\mathrm{true}}(\pi_k) = R_{\mathrm{proxy}}(\pi_k) \\ &\quad - \alpha\, D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_k \,\parallel\, \pi_0)^{\beta}, \qquad \Delta R_{\mathrm{verified}} \le \mathrm{Soundness}(\mathcal{E}) \end{aligned}$$

$$\begin{aligned} s_c = \frac{N_c}{\sum_{j=1}^{C} N_j}, \qquad \eta_{\mathrm{compute}}(\mathcal{L}) \\ &= \frac{R_{\mathrm{true}}(\mathcal{L}) - R_{\mathrm{true}}(\pi_0)}{\log_{10}\!\left( 1 + \mathrm{FLOPs}(\mathcal{L}) / \mathrm{FLOPs}(\pi_0) \right)} \end{aligned}$$

## 3. Governing Operators and Design Trade-Offs

In examining **EqSatL: A DSL for EqSat Strategies**, Chenyun Yin, Youwei Xiao, et al. (4 authors) trace how outer-loop interventions trade episodic flexibility relative to long-term persistence. Ruleset partitioning determines which rewrites may interact during EqSat search. In EqSatL, the rewrite vocabulary is partitioned into rulesets through semantic tags rather than plain rule lists, giving the synthesis process a more compact vocabulary for strategy construction. Schedule construction governs how ruleset interaction unfolds over time. EqSatL organizes execution as a flow tree that composes ruleset applications into ordered phases, sequences, and repeat regions. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17364v1#S4" aria-label="Source 8: 4. EqSatL: A DSL for EqSat Strategies">[8]</a></sup>

Shifting away from inference-time loops to **LLM-Guided Strategy Synthesis**, this review analyzes how self-generated rationales as well as process-level supervision consolidate onto durable policy updates. Once EqSat control is represented as an EqSatL artifact, the remaining challenge is to synthesize effective strategies over that artifact space while keeping search stable under e-graph growth. This section presents EggMind 's offline strategy synthesis methodology, which combines an agentic workflow for iterative strategy search, proof-derived rewrite motif caching for reusable evidence accumulation, and tractability guidance for controlling unstable rewrite interaction. Figure 5 shows one iteration of EggMind 's offline synthesis process, from iteration $i$ to $i+1$. At iteration $i$, the current search context is summarized by workflow memory that consists of a repository of EqSatL strategy artifacts, and a rewrite motif cache (detailed in Section 5.2) that stores reusable structural evidence from successful runs. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17364v1#S5" aria-label="Source 9: 5. LLM-Guided Strategy Synthesis">[9]</a></sup>

The authors use the same vectorization benchmark family as Isaria and Diospyros, covering both 2D-convolution ($2\mathrm{D}$) and matrix-multiplication ($\mathrm{MM}$) workloads.
