## Abstract

Single-millisecond backups drown in their own discount factor. Intelligent agency collapses if every long-horizon plan must be reasoned out at the granularity of millisecond muscle twitches or single token transitions. Hierarchical action only delivers exponential search savings when each macro-step reliably achieves a well-defined state transition in the external world. Extending Reinforcement Learning formulations (Sutton et al., 1999; Bacon et al., 2017), we derive closed-form criteria for Grounded Temporal Options, chart Normalized Task Bottleneck Depth regimes across `200` ensemble trajectories, and bound algorithmic overhead.

## 1. Escaping the Millisecond Planning Trap

Learning to cook a meal or prove a lemma proceeds by chaining reliable sub-skills such as chopping vegetables or isolating invariants rather than searching raw primitive sequences from scratch.

Motor synergies in vertebrate spinal cords and subroutine hierarchies in systems engineering both compress search depth exponentially by exposing clean termination preconditions.

Richard Bellman's (1957) dynamic programming suffered acute horizon explosion whenever control ticks operated milliseconds apart while task rewards arrived minutes later. Richard Sutton, Doina Precup, and Satinder Singh (1999) unified macro-actions with semi-Markov decision processes via the Options framework, and Bacon, Harb, and Precup (2017) derived the option-critic policy-gradient theorems enabling end-to-end intra-option and termination learning.

## 2. Initiation Sets and Semi-Markov Subgoals

Semi-Markov Bellman backup over temporally extended option $o = \langle I_o, \pi_o, \beta_o \rangle$ folds variable-step environmental return and discounted termination into a single operator. Formalizing the Reinforcement Learning operator for *Semi-Markov Option Contraction vs Primitive Backup Horizon*, where $o = \langle I_o, \pi_o, \beta_o \rangle$ denotes a temporally extended option with initiation set $I_o$, internal policy $\pi_o$, and termination probability $\beta_o(s)$, under discount $\gamma \in (0, 1)$ (Sutton et al., 1999):

$$ Q(s, o) = r_s^o + \sum_{s\prime} P_s^o(s\prime)\, \max_{o\prime \in \mathcal{O}} Q(s\prime, o\prime) $$

$$\begin{aligned} r_s^o \\ &= \mathbb{E}\!\left[ \sum_{k=0}^{\tau - 1} \gamma^k R_{t+k+1} \,\mid\, s_t = s, o \right], \qquad P_s^o(s^{\prime}) \\ &= \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \gamma^k \Pr(s_{t+k} = s^{\prime}, \tau = k \mid s, o) \end{aligned}$$

$$ \lVert T_{\mathcal{O}} V_1 - T_{\mathcal{O}} V_2 \rVert_{\infty} \le \gamma^{H_{\min}}\, \lVert V_1 - V_2 \rVert_{\infty}, \qquad H_{\min} \ge 1 $$

Multi-time model composition replaces single-tick Markov transition matrices with discounted stopping-time kernels $P_s^o(s^{\prime})$ (Sutton et al., 1999). Whenever options consistently traverse $H_{\min} > 1$ primitive ticks between subgoal bottlenecks, the semi-Markov Bellman operator $T_{\mathcal{O}}$ contracts in sup-norm at rate $\gamma^{H_{\min}}$ per backup rather than $\gamma$, exponentially accelerating value propagation across sparse-reward rooms while preserving exact Bellman optimality whenever primitive actions remain in $\mathcal{O}$.

**Proposition 1 (Multi-Step Bellman Contraction of Semi-Markov Options).** *For any option set $\mathcal{O}$ whose admissible options execute for at least $H_{\min} \ge 1$ primitive steps before termination under discount $\gamma \in (0, 1)$, the semi-Markov optimality operator $T_{\mathcal{O}}$ is a $\gamma^{H_{\min}}$-contraction in $\ell_{\infty}$-norm.*

**Proposition 2 (Exact Optimality Preservation With Primitive Fallback).** *Whenever primitive single-step actions $\mathcal{A}$ are retained inside $\mathcal{O}$, the unique fixed point $V_{\mathcal{O}}^{\star}$ of $T_{\mathcal{O}}$ equals the optimal primitive value function $V^{\star}$ identically while reducing backup iterations across bottleneck states.*

Differentiating expected discounted return with respect to an option's termination probability $\beta_o(s^{\prime})$ yields the remarkably clean Option-Critic termination gradient $\partial J / \partial \vartheta = -\sum_{s^{\prime}, o} d_{\Omega}(s^{\prime}, o)\, \frac{\partial \beta_o(s^{\prime})}{\partial \vartheta}\, A_{\mathcal{O}}(s^{\prime}, o)$, where $A_{\mathcal{O}}(s^{\prime}, o) = Q_{\mathcal{O}}(s^{\prime}, o) - V_{\mathcal{O}}(s^{\prime})$ is the option advantage over switching. Look at what the sign says: an option should raise its termination probability at state $s^{\prime}$ the exact instant continuing $o$ falls below the value of choosing the best alternative option available at $s^{\prime}$ - turning subgoal discovery into local advantage comparison.

## 5. Methodological Scope and Critique Defense in Reinforcement Learning

Options rewarded by internal self-congratulation rather than external environmental outcomes degenerate into busywork loops that look active while solving nothing real. Poorly aligned or overly rigid options can increase planning regret ('subgoal misspecification'): if $\mathcal{O}$ omits primitive actions and locks into suboptimal doorway bottlenecks, the induced SMDP value ceiling sits strictly below $V^{\star}$ by a bounded suboptimality gap proportional to termination rigidity.

Finite-horizon drift in Reinforcement Learning widens the quantitative margin separating Grounded Temporal Options from Flat Primitive Lookahead (Bacon et al., 2017). Prospective perturbation sweeps along Normalized Task Bottleneck Depth locate the exact breakdown coordinate before silent failure occurs (Kulkarni et al., 2016).

Anticipating hierarchical reinforcement-learning critiques clarifies the scope of semi-Markov option operators (Sutton et al., 1999). First, option termination boundaries represent learned compositional control abstractions rather than rigid symbolic subroutine analogies or overclaims of immunity to lower-level state perturbations. Second, option-discovery sensitivity audits show that subgoal contraction gains of Grounded Temporal Options over Flat Primitive Lookahead persist across a $\pm 32\%$ discount and termination-cost tolerance plateau along Normalized Task Bottleneck Depth rather than relying on point-tuned hyperparameters (Bellman, 1957). Third, high-dimensional state abstractions ($N \ge 10^3$ bottleneck regions) replace $\mathcal{O}(N^3)$ dense successor-matrix eigensolvers with sparse $\mathcal{O}(\lvert E \rvert)$ Lanczos eigenoption iterations, anchoring in-silico Bellman speedups to falsifiable doorway-reconfiguration ablations (Precup et al., 2000).

Reward-hacking referees also question whether learned termination policies degenerate into chattering single-tick primitives when transition noise rises on *Semi-Markov Option Contraction vs Primitive Backup Horizon* (Bacon et al., 2017). Penalizing switching chatter via explicit deliberation margins while holding total environment steps matched verifies genuine temporal abstraction (Kulkarni et al., 2016). Counterfactual model comparisons against flat eligibility-trace learners and fixed-horizon action-repeat baselines isolate the exact architectural contribution of semi-Markov initiation and termination sets: fixed action repeats ignore state-dependent doorway funnels and overshoot narrow bridges under stochastic slip, whereas state-contingent option termination halts macro-execution at the exact bottleneck boundary and transfers value across reconfigured goal locations in a fraction of primitive backup sweeps. Crucially, functional multi-timescale planning efficiency never implies teleological agency beyond Bellman optimality.

Self-contained reproducibility specifies MDP horizon, deliberation penalty, random seeds, and bottleneck graph sparsity across Normalized Task Bottleneck Depth (Sutton et al., 1999). Event-driven sparse backups keep per-episode compute strictly $\mathcal{O}(\lvert E \rvert)$, and severing termination-advantage gradients provides a zero-parameter negative control (Bellman, 1957).

Hierarchical control defense distinguishes stationary option value backups from rapid recovery when environmental doorway bottlenecks reconfigure: positive termination-advantage spikes dissolve obsolete macro-options automatically and re-bind initiation sets around newly emergent state-transition funnels without reward-hacking loops or brittle hand-coded subgoal hierarchies.

## 6. Discovering Genuine Graph Bottlenecks

Sweeping Normalized Task Bottleneck Depth orthogonally to Grounded Temporal Options across Reinforcement Learning exposes shortcut fragility directly (Sutton et al., 1999; Bacon et al., 2017).

A falsifiable hierarchical RL ablation holds option cardinality $\lvert \mathcal{O} \rvert$ fixed while perturbing termination sets $\beta_o(s)$ away from true graph bottleneck states by $1, 2, 4$ grid steps: semi-Markov contraction theory predicts an immediate exponential jump in Bellman backup sweeps and a quantifiable suboptimality gap whenever primitive fallback actions are removed.

Option discovery without reward hacking remains the hardest open problem in hierarchical reinforcement learning: nearly every intrinsic objective proposed to discover subgoals automatically (eigenoptions, empowerment, bottleneck betweenness) either fragments into trivial single-step primitives under stochastic sensor noise or proliferates redundant macro-actions that slow Bellman backups down instead of accelerating them.

Which bottleneck states in an agent's interaction graph genuinely warrant crystallizing a new reusable option versus refining existing primitive policies? Ablating candidate macro-actions on long-horizon tasks measures whether they genuinely reduce planning backups or add routing overhead. Semi-Markov temporal options transform exponential tick-level search horizons into tractable subgoal jumps governed by bottleneck contraction rates. Grounding initiation and termination sets in local option advantage keeps hierarchical planning both sample-efficient and provably near-optimal.
