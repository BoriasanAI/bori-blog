## Abstract

We present a formal and computational investigation of creativity as a structured dynamical system rather than an unanalyzable primitive. Moving beyond surface taxonomies of creative artifacts and historical etymologies, we decompose creativity into its interacting cognitive and systemic components (Amabile, 1983; Csikszentmihalyi, 1999), formalize the interplay of bisociative divergent exploration ($G_{\theta}$) and constraint-guided convergent verification ($E_{\phi}$), establish a multiplicative Novelty $\times$ Value evaluation model across six quantitative metrics, and formulate a five-stage closed-loop AI architecture culminating in a three-tier hierarchy from combinatorial ($A \oplus B \to C$) and transformational ($R_t \to R_t^{\prime}$) synthesis to self-evolving meta-rule creativity ($\Phi_{\mathrm{meta}}$).

## 1. Definition

A rigorous structural theory of creativity must begin by replacing informal intuition with an operational definition that is applied consistently across every analytical layer. Synthesizing convergent psychometric and computational formulations (Mumford, 2003; Sternberg & Lubart, 1999; Runco & Jaeger, 2012), we define creativity as the capacity of an epistemic system to transform a structured knowledge state $K_t$ into a candidate artifact or hypothesis $y$ that jointly satisfies two non-compensatory conditions: **Novelty** ($\mathcal{N}(y \mid K_t) > \tau_{\mathrm{nov}}$, requiring non-trivial structural divergence from previously stored exemplars) and **Value** ($\mathcal{V}(y \mid \mathcal{G}, K_t) > \tau_{\mathrm{val}}$, requiring functional utility, explanatory power, and internal coherence with respect to a target problem or aesthetic frame $\mathcal{G}$).

A common analytical error in introductory treatments is to confuse a **taxonomy of creative products** with the **generative mechanism of creativity**. Classifying outputs into intangible products of creativity (ideas, scientific theories, literary works, musical compositions, and jokes) versus physical products (inventions, culinary dishes, pieces of jewelry, costumes, and paintings) describes how creative products may be classified as either intangible or physical at the surface medium of instantiation, not how creativity operates internally. Whether a theoretical physicist unifies two disparate field equations or a chef combines chemically complementary ingredients into a new dish, the underlying cognitive architecture executes the same six substrate-invariant operations: **(1) structured representation and storage of prior knowledge $K_t$, (2) cross-domain associative linking across distant concepts, (3) combinatorial or transformational generation of candidate structures, (4) multi-criteria evaluation of novelty and value, (5) feedback-driven iterative revision, and (6) consolidation of verified structures back into long-term memory**.

Similarly, **historical etymology must be strictly separated from causal mechanism**. Tracing the English word *creativity* to the classical Latin *creare* ("to bring forth, make") and its cognate root *crescere* ("to let things grow, arise incrementally"), or noting that pre-modern traditions—such as Plato's *Republic* (597b–e), which framed visual art as *mimesis* (imitation of eternal Forms) while reserving *poiesis* for poetry, and 14th-century uses in Chaucer's *The Parson's Tale* restricting creation to divine *creatio ex nihilo*—tracks how cultures attributed *agency*, not how cognition computes novel structure. Even after Renaissance humanism and Enlightenment theorists (Thomas Hobbes, 1651; William Duff, 1767) relocated imagination inside human cognition, cross-cultural empirical studies demonstrate that lexical framing varies widely: research in East Asian contexts (Niu & Sternberg, 2002; Rudowicz & Yue, 2000) emphasizes progressive cultivation and social-ethical contribution over radical rupture, while Mpofu et al. (2006) documented that 27 of 28 surveyed African languages encode creative action through domain-specific skill and wisdom terms rather than a single abstract noun. These philological and sociocultural findings show that cultures parameterize the *value function* $\mathcal{V}$ differently, whereas a structural theory must answer six foundational questions: (1) What components constitute a creative system? (2) What causal relations bind them? (3) What emergent phenomena arise from their interaction? (4) What boundary separates creativity from random noise? (5) How are genuinely unprecedented concepts synthesized? and (6) How can a creative system recursively improve its own creative capacity?

## 2. Components

Addressing the first two structural questions requires decomposing creativity into interacting subsystems whose causal dependencies are explicit. In **Teresa M. Amabile's (1983, 1996) Componential Theory of Creativity**, creative performance arises from the multiplicative confluence of three within-individual components and one contextual component: (1) **domain-relevant skills** (the structured repertoire $K_t$ of factual knowledge, technical procedures, and domain constraints), (2) **creativity-relevant processes** (cognitive heuristics for suspending functional fixedness, shifting perspective, and traversing low-prior search paths), (3) **intrinsic task motivation** (endogenous curiosity and persistence that sustain exploration when immediate extrinsic reinforcement is absent), and (4) the **social environment**. Extending this individual architecture to the macro-level, **Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's (1996, 1999) Systems Model** demonstrates that creativity is not an isolated mental event but a coupled dynamical triad $(\mathcal{I}, \mathcal{D}, \mathcal{F})$ connecting the **Individual** ($\mathcal{I}$, who generates candidate variations), the symbolic **Domain** ($\mathcal{D}$, the preserved grammar and rule set $R_t$ of a discipline), and the social **Field** ($\mathcal{F}$, the community of expert evaluators that filters and retains validated innovations into $D_{t+1}$).

Within cognitive and psychometric architectures, these components map onto distinct processing capacities. Under the **Cattell–Horn–Carroll (CHC)** model of intelligence, creative production is causally anchored in broad **long-term storage and retrieval ($G_{lr}$)**—specifically ideational fluency, associational fluency, and semantic flexibility (Silvia et al., 2013)—modulated by Colin Martindale's (1999) cortical arousal dynamics, where broadened attentional defocusing during early exploration gives way to accelerated executive processing during verification. Crucially, **Dual-Process Theory** (Kaufman, 2011) and **Sébastien Helie and Ron Sun's (2010) Explicit–Implicit Interaction (EII) Theory** show that creative components operate across two coupled representational levels: implicit, associative, sub-symbolic representations ($K_{\mathrm{imp}}$) that support parallel distant spreading activation, and explicit, rule-governed symbolic representations ($K_{\mathrm{exp}}$) that enforce logical verification. Empirical **Threshold Theory** further clarifies the causal relation between intelligence and creativity: general analytic intelligence acts as a necessary prerequisite gate (correlating moderately at $r \approx 0.30\text{–}0.40$ up to an IQ threshold near $120$), above which variance in creative achievement is governed by associative flexibility, intrinsic motivation, and evaluative discipline across all four scales of **Kaufman and Beghetto's (2009) Four-C hierarchy** (*mini-c* personal insight, *little-c* everyday problem solving, *Pro-C* professional mastery, and *Big-C* historical paradigm shifts).

## 3. Mechanisms

How do these components interact to generate structures that did not previously exist in $K_t$? The core generative mechanism is the interplay of **remote associative search**, **structural blending**, and **divergent–convergent oscillation**. In *The Act of Creation*, **Arthur Koestler (1964)** formalized **bisociation** as the intersection of two previously orthogonal matrices of thought $M_1$ and $M_2$ along a shared relational invariant, in contrast to routine associative thought confined to a single matrix. Cognitive science subsequently generalized bisociation into **Conceptual Blending** (Fauconnier & Turner, 2002) and **Ronald Finke, Thomas Ward, and Steven Smith's (1992) Geneplore Model**, which decomposes synthesis into a **generative phase**—constructing malleable mental representations termed *preinventive structures* via retrieval, analogy, and categorical combination—and an **exploratory phase** that interprets and tests those structures under novel functional constraints. This bidirectional cycle explains the emergent dynamics of **Graham Wallas's (1926) five-stage model** (*preparation* $\to$ *incubation* $\to$ *intimation* $\to$ *illumination* $\to$ *verification*): during *incubation*, conscious executive inhibition relaxes, allowing implicit EII spreading activation to traverse low-probability cross-domain bridges until a coherent structural alignment crosses the threshold of conscious awareness as *illumination*.

This mechanistic framework explains *why* **J. P. Guilford's (1967) Structure-of-Intellect psychometric tasks** serve as valid operational probes of creative cognition rather than arbitrary word games. When Guilford's Alternative Uses Task asks a participant to list unusual uses for everyday objects such as **bricks**, the task directly measures the capacity to **inhibit dominant high-prior functional associations** ($P(\text{masonry} \mid \text{brick}) \gg P(\text{thermal ballast or pigment} \mid \text{brick})$)—overcoming *functional fixedness* and expanding the *associative horizon* by decomposing the object into lower-level physical features (mass, rectangular geometry, porous clay absorption) and re-binding them to distant goal schemas. Similarly, generating original **story titles** measures *abstraction and cross-register compression*, while enumerating consequences of unexpected counterfactual events such as the **loss of gravity** ($\mathrm{do}(g = 0)$) measures *multi-hop counterfactual causal propagation* across unobserved regions of a world model.

Most importantly for both psychology and artificial intelligence, **creativity must be strictly distinguished from divergent thinking** (Runco, 2008). **Divergent thinking** ($G_{\theta}$) is the exploratory operator that expands the support of candidate ideas $H_t = \lbrace y_1, \dots, y_M \rbrace$ across multiple directions (fluency, flexibility, and statistical infrequency). **Convergent thinking** ($E_{\phi}$) is the evaluative selection operator that subjects $H_t$ to logical, empirical, and goal constraints to isolate viable solutions. **Creativity** is the coupled composite operator $E_{\phi} \circ G_{\theta}$: an unconstrained generator without convergent verification produces hallucination or noise, whereas a convergent optimizer without divergent expansion remains trapped in local optima.

## 4. Evaluation

To answer the fourth foundational question—how genuine creativity is formally separated from random generation—we must specify an explicit mathematical evaluation model. A stochastic generator that randomly permutes symbols or graph edges achieves maximal statistical surprise ($\mathcal{N} \to \max$) but zero functional utility or structural coherence. Consequently, creative quality $\mathcal{C}(y \mid K_t, \mathcal{G})$ cannot be modeled as an additive sum in which high novelty compensates for nonsensical output; it requires a **multiplicative dual-axis formulation** gated by structural coherence:

$$\mathcal{C}(y \mid K_t, \mathcal{G}) = \underbrace{D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(P(y \mid \mathcal{G}) \;\parallel\; P_{K_t}(y)\right)^{\alpha}}_{\text{Epistemic Novelty } \mathcal{N}(y \mid K_t)} \cdot \underbrace{U(y \mid \mathcal{G})^{\beta} \cdot \sigma\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{Coh}(y, K_t) - \tau_{\mathrm{coh}}}{\kappa}\right)}_{\text{Verified Functional Value } \mathcal{V}(y \mid \mathcal{G}, K_t)}$$

Here $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P(y \mid \mathcal{G}) \parallel P_{K_t}(y))$ quantifies how far candidate $y$ departs from the system's prior expectation distribution $P_{K_t}$, $U(y \mid \mathcal{G}) \in [0, 1]$ measures domain utility, and $\sigma((\mathrm{Coh} - \tau_{\mathrm{coh}})/\kappa)$ is a sharp sigmoid verification gate enforcing logical and physical consistency above threshold $\tau_{\mathrm{coh}}$. Under this law, both rote reproduction ($\mathcal{N} = 0 \implies \mathcal{C} = 0$) and ungrounded random generation ($\mathrm{Coh} \ll \tau_{\mathrm{coh}} \text{ or } U = 0 \implies \mathcal{C} \approx 0$) collapse to zero. For an AI system to operationalize this criterion across scientific, engineering, and linguistic domains, evaluation must be decomposed into six measurable dimensions:

| Evaluation Metric | Target Construct | Formal Operationalization | AI Verification & Measurement Protocol |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Novelty ($\mathcal{N}$) | Differentiation from prior knowledge base $K_t$ | Information gain $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P(y \mid c) \parallel P_{K_t}(y))$ or structural graph distance $1 - \max_{k \in K_t} \mathrm{sim}(y, k)$ | Semantic embedding distance & automated originality scoring ($r = 0.60\text{–}0.72$ with human raters) |
| Diversity ($\mathcal{D}$) | Breadth across distinct conceptual categories | Mean pairwise distance $\frac{2}{M(M-1)}\sum_{i<j} d_{\mathrm{sem}}(y_i, y_j)$ over candidate set $H_t$ | Subtree coverage across Guilford flexibility dimensions and ontology domains |
| Usefulness ($\mathcal{U}$) | Functional utility toward goal specification $\mathcal{G}$ | Normalized objective satisfaction $R_{\mathcal{G}}(y) \in [0, 1]$ under task constraints | Empirical benchmark validation, predictive accuracy gain, or domain rubric |
| Coherence ($\mathrm{Coh}$) | Internal logical and causal consistency | Constraint satisfaction ratio $\frac{1}{\lvert C_{\mathrm{inv}} \rvert}\sum_{c \in C_{\mathrm{inv}}} \mathbb{I}[y \models c]$ | Dual-critique structural verification, axiom/type checking, and cycle consistency |
| Feasibility ($\mathcal{F}$) | Physical, resource, and computational realizability | Bounded execution indicator $\mathbb{I}[\mathrm{Cost}(y) \le B_{\max}] \cdot \exp(-\lambda \,\mathrm{Risk}(y))$ | Sandbox execution, algorithmic complexity bounds, and world-model simulation |
| Adaptability ($A_{\mathrm{adapt}}$) | Iterative improvement rate under feedback | Positive revision slope $\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \max(0, \mathcal{C}(y^{(t)}) - \mathcal{C}(y^{(t-1)}))$ | Multi-turn counterfactual repair gain $\Delta \mathcal{C}$ after verifier critique |

As the table demonstrates, no single scalar proxy suffices in isolation: domain-specific creative regimes weight these six axes differently (for example, formal theorem synthesis enforces $\mathrm{Coh} = 1.0$, whereas exploratory hypothesis generation prioritizes $\mathcal{N}$, $\mathcal{D}$, and $A_{\mathrm{adapt}}$), yet every valid creative regime requires non-zero survival across both the novelty and verified-value factors.

## 5. Learning

A static generator-evaluator pair whose knowledge base $K_t$ remains frozen eventually exhausts its reachable combinations. Genuine creative agency therefore requires a **closed learning loop** in which evaluated outcomes restructure memory and refine future search. In computational theory, **Jürgen Schmidhuber's (2010) Formal Theory of Creativity and Intrinsic Motivation** models this drive through **compression progress**: let $L_t(D_{1:t})$ denote the description length (in bits) required by the agent's internal world model $K_t$ to encode interaction history $D_{1:t}$. The intrinsic curiosity reward for generating or observing a novel pattern is the first difference:

$$r_t^{\mathrm{int}}(y) = L_t(D_{1:t} \cup \lbrace y \rbrace) - L_{t+1}(D_{1:t} \cup \lbrace y \rbrace) = \Delta L_t(y) > 0$$

Maximizing $\Delta L_t(y)$ compels the system to seek out regularities that are currently unfamiliar yet learnable—automatically steering attention away from both trivial, already-compressed routines ($\Delta L_t = 0$) and incompressible white noise ($\Delta L_t = 0$). In cognitive psychology, **Liane Gabora's (2005, 2017) Honing Theory** captures the macroscopic consequence of this update: creative work is not merely the external production of an artifact $y^{\ast}$, but the self-organizing, self-mending restructuring of the agent's entire internal worldview $W_t \to W_{t+1}$ as task constraints and conceptual schemas iteratively reshape one another. At the neurobiological level, **Lin and Vartanian's (2018) neuroeconomic framework** and **Sternberg and Lubart's (1991) Investment Theory** show that this learning loop operates as a utility-maximizing exploration–exploitation policy mediated by locus coeruleus–norepinephrine (LC-NE) gain control and default-mode / executive network coupling, enabling the agent to invest search budget in undervalued, low-prior structural hypotheses and consolidate high-return discoveries into long-term relational memory.

## 6. Computational Model

Integrating the definitions, components, mechanisms, evaluation metrics, and learning dynamics established in Sections 1–5 yields an explicit **Five-Stage Closed-Loop Computational Model of Creativity** directly implementable in artificial general intelligence:

1. **Stage ① Knowledge Base ($K_t = (V_t, E_t, R_t)$)**: Encodes grounded concepts $V_t$, typed relational edges $E_t$, episodic traces, and active generative/inferential rules $R_t$.
2. **Stage ② Associative Search & Distant Exploration ($\mathcal{A}$)**: Executes multi-hop graph traversal, similarity projection, and perspective shifting to retrieve cross-domain concept tuples $(u, v) \in V_t^2$ that share deep structural homomorphisms despite large surface distance.
3. **Stage ③ Combinatorial & Transformational Generation ($G_{\theta}$)**: Synthesizes candidate hypotheses $H_t = \lbrace y_1, \dots, y_M \rbrace$ via conceptual blending, counterfactual intervention ($\mathrm{do}(x)$), and constraint relaxation.
4. **Stage ④ Multi-Axis Evaluation & Verification ($E_{\phi}$)**: Filters $H_t$ across Novelty, Diversity, Usefulness, Coherence, and Feasibility to select $y^{\ast} = \arg\max_{y \in H_t} \mathcal{C}(y \mid K_t, \mathcal{G})$.
5. **Stage ⑤ Feedback Learning & Schema Accumulation ($\Phi_{\mathrm{update}}$)**: Uses verification residuals, compression progress $\Delta L_t$, and environmental feedback to revise $y^{\ast}$, insert validated concepts into $V_{t+1}$, and close the loop back to Stage ①.

To answer the sixth and deepest structural question—*whether a creative system is limited to recombining fixed knowledge or can transform the very structure of its own thought*—we extend **Margaret A. Boden's (1990, 2004)** taxonomy of combinatorial, exploratory, and transformational creativity into a **Three-Tier Hierarchy of Creative Evolution**:

$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Tier 1 (Combinatorial Creativity):} \quad & y_{\mathrm{comb}} = A \oplus_{R_t} B, \qquad A, B \in V_t, \quad R_{t+1} = R_t \\[4pt]
\text{Tier 2 (Transformational Creativity):} \quad & R_t \xrightarrow{\;\Psi_{\mathrm{trans}}\;}
R_t^{\prime}, \qquad y_{\mathrm{trans}} \in \mathcal{S}(R_t^{\prime}) \setminus \mathcal{S}(R_t) \\[4pt]
\text{Tier 3 (Self-Evolving Meta-Rule Creativity):} \quad & (R_{t+1}, E_{\phi,t+1}) = \Phi_{\mathrm{meta}}\!\left(R_t, E_{\phi,t}, \nabla_{R}\mathcal{C}(H_t)\right)
\end{aligned}$$

| Creative Evolution Tier | Generative Operation | Rule & Space Dynamics | Distinguishing Capability in Cognitive & AGI Systems |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 1: Combinatorial Creativity | $y = A \oplus_{R_t} B$ (cross-domain blending of existing concepts) | Fixed conceptual space $\mathcal{S}(R_t)$ and fixed evaluation operator $E_{\phi,t}$ | Bridges distant ontology nodes (bisociation / metaphor) without altering underlying grammar or axioms |
| Tier 2: Transformational Creativity | $R_t \xrightarrow{\Psi_{\mathrm{trans}}} R_t^{\prime}$ (dropping, inverting, or relaxing constitutive rules) | Expands or warps the conceptual space $\mathcal{S}(R_t^{\prime}) \supset \mathcal{S}(R_t)$ (Boden, 2004) | Synthesizes structures that were formally unreachable under prior representational constraints |
| Tier 3: Self-Evolving (Meta-Rule) Creativity | $(R_{t+1}, E_{\phi,t+1}) = \Phi_{\mathrm{meta}}(R_t, E_{\phi,t}, \nabla_{R}\mathcal{C})$ | Co-evolves generative rules $R_t$, evaluation criteria $E_{\phi,t}$, and cognitive architecture | Generates new reasoning operators, verifies them against empirical feedback, and integrates them into its own mind |

In **Tier 1: Combinatorial Creativity**, the system combines existing representations $A$ and $B$ into $C$ under unchanged rules $R_t$. In **Tier 2: Transformational Creativity**, the system alters or drops a constitutive constraint of $R_t$ itself (as non-Euclidean geometry dropped Playfair's parallel postulate or Schoenbergian atonality relaxed tonal resolution), rendering previously impossible regions of conceptual space reachable. In **Tier 3: Self-Evolving Creativity**—the defining horizon for autonomous AGI—the system synthesizes candidate *meta-rules* ($R^{\prime}$), evaluates whether those new rules improve long-horizon creative yield $\mathcal{C}$ and adaptability $A_{\mathrm{adapt}}$, and writes verified rules back into its own reasoning and evaluation architecture.

## 7. Limitations

Despite its explanatory power, any formal model of creativity confronts three fundamental theoretical and engineering limitations:

1. **Combinatorial Explosion vs. Search Tractability**: Over an ontology of $\lvert V_t \rvert = N$ concepts and $\lvert R_t \rvert = K$ transformation operators, unconstrained $m$-step synthesis scales as $O((N \cdot K)^m)$. Without learned structural priors and sparse associative routing (Stage ②), exhaustive divergent generation becomes computationally intractable long before reaching deep multi-hop analogies.
2. **Evaluator Myopia and the Transformational Paradox**: When a Tier 2 or Tier 3 transformation modifies the constitutive rules $R_t \to R_t^{\prime}$, an unadapted evaluator $E_{\phi,t}$ calibrated solely on legacy axioms may misclassify a genuine paradigm shift as a coherence violation ($\mathrm{Coh} < \tau_{\mathrm{coh}}$). Resolving this tension requires co-evolving $E_{\phi}$ via predictive compression gain ($\Delta L_t > 0$) and external empirical testing rather than static stylistic templates.
3. **Self-Reward Collapse (Goodhart's Law in Tier 3 Self-Evolution)**: If a self-evolving agent is permitted to modify its own value function $E_{\phi,t+1}$ without an external grounding boundary, it can trivially maximize $\mathcal{C}$ by lowering $\tau_{\mathrm{coh}}$ or rewarding degenerate self-patterns. Consequently, Tier 3 meta-rule updates must remain strictly anchored to independent structural verification and real-world falsifiability.

## 8. Conclusion

Treating creativity as a structured concept rather than an unanalyzable primitive requires moving beyond surface classifications of creative artifacts and historical etymologies to specify the causal machinery that generates novelty with value. By decomposing creative cognition into its within-individual and systemic components (Amabile; Csikszentmihalyi), formalizing the interplay of bisociative divergent search and convergent verification (Guilford; Koestler; Finke et al.), establishing a six-axis quantitative evaluation matrix gated by $\mathcal{N} \times \mathcal{V}$, and extending Margaret Boden's conceptual-space transformations to **Tier 3: Self-Evolving (Meta-Rule) Creativity**, this framework provides both a rigorous theoretical synthesis and an actionable blueprint for autonomous creative intelligence.
