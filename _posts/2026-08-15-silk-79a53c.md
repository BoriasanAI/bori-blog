# Silk: Structure and Consequence

To ask what silk really is turns out to be harder than it looks. The working answer, that silk is a natural protein fiber, some forms of which can be woven or knitted into textiles, carries us some way, but the structure underneath deserves a closer look.

What complicates the picture is this: the protein fiber of silk is composed mainly of fibroin (75-80%) and a smaller coating of sericin (20-25%). The idea is less tidy than its textbook form suggests, and that untidiness is precisely where its interest lies.

A further point follows from the first. It is most commonly produced from the cocoons of certain insect larvae, and once that is granted, several apparent puzzles resolve into ordinary consequences of the same structure.

If a single thread runs through all of this, it is that silk rewards attention. The closer one looks, the more structure appears, and that, in the end, is the point.
