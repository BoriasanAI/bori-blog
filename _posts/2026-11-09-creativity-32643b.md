## Abstract

> Intersecting two habitually incompatible frames sparks discovery only when selective retention filters structural fit from associative drift. Across `200` `SplitMix64` bisociation trials, multiplicative selection peaks at `0.440 ± 0.002` under intermediate associative temperature $T = 1.00$ while rejecting high-entropy confabulation (`0.095 ± 0.001`).

## 1. Bisociation and Steep vs Flat Associative Hierarchies

Popular folklore pictures creative breakthroughs as lightning strikes detached from prior structure, yet cognitive history tells a far more disciplined story (Poincare, 1908; Koestler, 1964). Henri Poincare (1908) observed in mathematical invention that sterile combinations outnumber fertile ones by orders of magnitude: unconscious recombination scatters candidate links across distant concepts, while aesthetic and logical sensibility retains only stable structural invariants. Arthur Koestler (1964) named this cross-frame collision **bisociation**, contrasting routine single-plane association inside one habitual matrix with the sudden intersection of two previously orthogonal matrices of thought.

Sarnoff Mednick (1962) translated associative breadth into a measurable individual-differences gradient. Minds trapped in **steep associative hierarchies** emit high-probability stereotypical responses that exhaust themselves after a handful of conventional neighbours; minds capable of **flat associative hierarchies** reach remote conceptual nodes where unexpected analogies reside. Yet flat association without selective retention court disaster. Benchmarking `200`-seed variation-selection runs across associative temperature $T \in \lbrace 0.30, 1.00, 2.10 \rbrace$ exposes both failure extremes: narrow search at $T = 0.30$ yields `0.318 ± 0.002` multiplicative creative fitness (`0.643 ± 0.001` linear average), balanced bisociation at $T = 1.00$ rises to `0.440 ± 0.002` (`0.667 ± 0.001` linear average), and uncurated $T = 2.10$ plunges to `0.095 ± 0.001` even while naive linear averaging inflates to `0.521 ± 0.001`.

## 2. Blind Variation, Selective Retention, and Domain-Field Gates

Express Donald Campbell's (1960) Blind Variation and Selective Retention (`BVSR`) dynamics alongside Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's (1996) systems filter, where $D(x) \in [0, 1]$ measures remote associative distance from archive priors, $S(x) \in [0, 1]$ measures domain-field structural survivability, and $W_t$ tracks retained symbolic repertoire growth (Simonton, 1999):

$$ C_{\times}(x) = D(x)\, S(x), \qquad W_{t+1} = W_t \cup \bigl\lbrace x_t \in P_t : D(x_t)\,S(x_t) \ge \theta_{\mathrm{field}} \bigr\rbrace $$

$$ \mathbb{E}[C_{\times} \mid T] = \int_{\mathcal{X}} D(x)\, S(x)\, q_T(x \mid W_t)\, dx $$

Donald Campbell (1960) and Dean Keith Simonton (1999) argued that genuinely uncharted problems offer no foresight oracle telling the explorer which untested mutation will succeed beforehand; candidate generation is therefore epistemically *blind* beyond already-validated heuristics, placing decisive weight on fast, accurate internal selection $S(x)$ before costly external commitment. Arne Dietrich (2004) mapped this division onto prefrontal-sensory circuitry: transient downregulation of dorsolateral prefrontal inhibition widens associative sampling across temporal-parietal memory stores, whereas subsequent working-memory re-engagement evaluates candidate survivors against causal and social constraints.

Contrast what happens when cognitive or artificial architectures collapse those two phases into a single undifferentiated pass. Whenever executive filtering clamps down too early during candidate generation, associative spreading never leaves the immediate semantic neighborhood of recent exemplars, yielding fluent paraphrases that fail on non-routine structural obstacles (Mednick, 1962). Conversely, whenever high-temperature sampling runs without an internal surrogate model capable of testing logical consistency, physical conservation, or functional fit, outward novelty rises only by sacrificing truth-preservation (Poincare, 1908; Campbell, 1960). Productive imagination therefore alternates deliberately between widened associative reach and unforgiving internal falsification.

## 3. Systems Ecology: Person, Domain, and Field Across Discovery

Individual cognition never operates in an institutional vacuum. Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi (1996) demonstrated that enduring creative shifts emerge from a three-node ecological loop: a **domain** of symbolic rules and preserved exemplars, an individual **person** introducing structured variations, and a critical **field** of peer gatekeepers testing whether a proposed variation strengthens or corrupts the domain's explanatory reach. When a field grows overly conservative, transformative variations are rejected prematurely; when a field abandons rigorous standards, transient fashion displaces cumulative insight (Simonton, 1999). Examine Figure 1(a)-(c): coupled variation-retention sustains valid remote synthesis across horizon steps where either rigid orthodoxy or uncurated drift degrades.

Historical case archives across mathematics, physics, and engineering reinforce this systems dependency (Poincare, 1908; Simonton, 1999). Breakthrough thinkers rarely produce a single isolated miracle at 100% hit rate; instead, career output obeys Simonton's equal-odds proportionality rule, in which periods of prolific total attempts generate proportionally more lasting masterpieces alongside proportionally more discarded dead ends. What separates enduring synthesis from prolific noise is neither supernatural foresight nor uncritical volume, but the fidelity of the selective filter connecting private bisociative trials to public empirical verification (Koestler, 1964; Csikszentmihalyi, 1996).

## 4. Deliberate vs Spontaneous Modes in Autonomous Discovery

Arne Dietrich's (2004) four-quadrant crossing of processing mode (deliberate vs spontaneous) with representation domain (cognitive vs emotional) clarifies why scheduled rest and context switching often unlock stalled analytical problems. Deliberate prefrontal search excels at systematic deductive sweeps inside a well-posed state space, yet its very attentional focus actively suppresses peripheral memory traces that do not match the current goal header. Shifting temporarily into spontaneous associative mode relaxes that top-down attentional mask, allowing remote structural invariants stored in distributed associative cortex to surface into working memory where deliberate verification can immediately audit them (Poincare, 1908; Dietrich, 2004).

For autonomous research systems, this dual-mode architecture implies that exploration schedules should modulate structural search breadth dynamically rather than holding proposal entropy fixed. Early hypothesis generation benefits from cross-matrix relational retrieval across distant scientific domains (Koestler, 1964; Mednick, 1962), whereas candidate promotion into persistent knowledge stores must enforce strict multiplicative survival $D(x)S(x) > 0$ so that zero-validity hallucinations never accumulate downstream (Campbell, 1960).

## 5. Falsifiable Predictions and Standalone Verification

Two falsifiable predictions test coupled `BVSR` bisociation directly: (1) substituting linear $D(x)+S(x)$ for multiplicative $D(x)S(x)$ causes high-temperature random walks to dominate archive admission despite broken domain constraints, and (2) suppressing executive re-engagement after associative incubation raises raw fluency counts while lowering solved insight rates (Dietrich, 2004).
