# Toward a Definition of Planning

## Abstract

This paper examines planning as a structured concept rather than a primitive. Taking its standard definition as a point of departure, it isolates the properties that give the concept its internal organization and argues that these properties, not the label, are what the concept's explanatory work depends on.

## 1. Introduction

The concept of planning is widely invoked yet unevenly specified. A working definition holds that planning is the process of thinking regarding the activities required to achieve a desired goal. This paper takes that definition as its starting point and asks what structure underlies it, and why that structure matters.

## 2. Structure and Properties

A defining property is the following: Planning is based on foresight, the fundamental capacity for mental time travel. This property is not incidental; it constrains how planning may be instantiated and marks it off from adjacent concepts with which it is frequently conflated.

## 3. Conclusion

This analysis has treated planning not as a self-evident given but as a structured concept whose properties can be stated and examined. The organization surveyed above suggests that its apparent simplicity conceals a definite internal structure, one that rewards further and more formal study.
