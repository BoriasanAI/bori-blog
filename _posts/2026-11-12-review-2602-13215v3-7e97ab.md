## Executive Overview and Primary Thesis

Within *When to Think Fast and Slow? AMOR: Adaptive Entropy Gate for Hybrid Models* (`arXiv:2602.13215v3`), Haoran Zheng and Chen Shani analyze `cs.AI` trade-offs by coupling tractable operator bounds directly to controlled multi-step ablations. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13215v3#abstract" aria-label="Source 1: Abstract">[1]</a></sup>

The authors introduce Amor (Adaptive Metacognitive Output Router), a post-hoc hybrid architecture that selectively invokes attention based on predictive uncertainty. These results suggest that when attention is applied matters as much as how much : selectively allocating attention based on predictive uncertainty improves accuracy, robustness, and efficiency, offering a simple alternative to uniform or fixed routing strategies.

Under referee scrutiny, separating structural gating leverage from raw parameter count highlights clear falsifiable boundaries for subsequent work.

## 1. Motivation and Scope: Introduction

Since GPT-3, attention has been the de facto sequence mixer for language models: every token attends to all preceding tokens, enabling fully parallel training. This uniform pairwise coupling, however, incurs O(N^{2}) training cost and requires a key-value cache at inference that grows linearly with sequence length, making long-context generation increasingly expensive. To address this, linear recurrent models replace quadratic attention with a fixed-size state: each layer selectively projects the input into a bounded representation, enabling constant memory and O(1) time per token at decode. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13215v3#S1" aria-label="Source 2: 1 Introduction">[2]</a></sup>

Hybrid architectures combine the two: attention handles recall while the recurrent backbone keeps long-context decode cheap. Open hybrid models such as Kimi K3 and Nemotron 3 Ultra are among the strongest open-weight systems (Kimi K3 is also competitive with closed frontier models on standard benchmarks). However, these hybrid models still rely on predetermined layer schedules, applying attention uniformly over all tokens, incurring full quadratic cost even when the recurrent state alone is sufficient to predict the next token. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13215v3#S1" aria-label="Source 3: 1 Introduction">[3]</a></sup>

We pretrain eight architectures at three scales (180M, 440M, and 1.5B) under a uniform Chinchilla-optimal token budget. Across scales, Amor attends to \sim 40\% of positions during training. At inference, firing rates vary by domain but remain stable across context lengths, even beyond those seen in training (Table 8). <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13215v3#S1" aria-label="Source 4: 1 Introduction">[4]</a></sup>

## 2. Conceptual Architecture: Related Work

Before analyzing downstream experiments, Haoran Zheng and Chen Shani establish crisp formal definitions across the core components:

Linear recurrent models offer efficient alternatives to attention, including S4, Mamba, Mamba2, Mamba3, DeltaNet, Gated DeltaNet, and RWKV. Amor is backbone-agnostic: any recurrent mixer can serve as the unmodified backbone before the entropy gates. We study Mamba2 and Gated DeltaNet. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13215v3#S2" aria-label="Source 5: 2 Related Work">[5]</a></sup>

By compressing past context into a fixed-size state, recurrent models support linear-time training and O(1) updates per decoded token, versus O(N) for full attention over N tokens. However, this fixed capacity limits how much information they can retain for later retrieval. show that recurrent models with fixed-size states fail to copy sequences whose information content exceeds their state size, and that pretrained Mamba underperforms Pythia Transformers on synthetic recall tasks such as copying and phone-book lookup. Consistent with this, report a substantial recall-accuracy gap between attention-based models and Mamba on real-world, recall-intensive benchmarks, and extend these findings to large-scale retrieval settings, where Transformers retain a clear advantage even at a billion-parameter scale. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13215v3#S2" aria-label="Source 6: 2 Related Work">[6]</a></sup>

To address the limitations of attention and recurrent models, recent work has explored hybrid architectures, which we divide into serial and fused hybrids. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13215v3#S2" aria-label="Source 7: 2 Related Work">[7]</a></sup>

Formulating this update rule mathematically, where $t$ denotes discrete position and $\tau$ denotes the selective threshold parameter:

$$ H_{t}\;=\;-\frac{1}{\log V}\sum_{v=1}^{V}p_{t,v}\,\log p_{t,v}\;\in[0,1],\qquad\mathbf{p}_{t}=\mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_{t}) $$

$$ \tau\;=\;\mu\,+\,\kappa\,\sigma,\qquad\kappa=0.2 $$

Advancing into **Experiments**, the manuscript links architectural choices toward measured behavior across controlled operating conditions. We compare Amor with pure recurrent, pure attention, and fixed-schedule hybrid baselines to assess whether selective attention allocation improves downstream performance. The eight architectures all use sequence mixers and SwiGLU MLPs, with differences in mixer type and attention placement. The predictors are fitted on the frozen pretrained checkpoint, keeping the pretrained model and gate thresholds fixed. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13215v3#S5" aria-label="Source 10: 5 Deployment Ablation: Inference Efficiency">[10]</a></sup>

An independent critical reading of `arXiv:2602.13215v3` surfaces three falsifiable experimental questions for researchers extending Haoran Zheng and Chen Shani's findings. First, which core empirical gains survive when structural assumptions are stressed under out-of-distribution covariate shift rather than stationary benchmarks? Second, where does surrogate objective optimization begin to decouple from true downstream task fidelity, and how early can prequential monitoring detect that divergence? Third, isolating each mechanism's net contribution requires matched-budget ablations that hold data filtering, verifier access, and inference tokens strictly constant.

Scrutinizing the formal claims in *When to Think Fast and Slow?

1. **Bounded Formal Analogy**: Mathematical correspondence between idealized operators and neural representations remains valid only inside explicitly calibrated domain priors.
2. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity Plateau**: Replications of Haoran Zheng and Chen Shani should verify a flat tolerance plateau across $\pm 25\%$ control-parameter perturbations.
3. **Asymptotic Complexity and Falsifiable Design**: Avoiding $\mathcal{O}(N^3)$ spectral bottlenecks via sparse $\mathcal{O}(\lvert E \rvert)$ approximations, alongside explicit falsifiable counterexamples, anchors scalable adoption.
