## Abstract

In *CausalForge: A Formally Grounded, Self-Improving Agentic Framework for Automated Research in Causal Inference* (`arXiv:2607.22511v1`), Jiyuan Tan and Vasilis Syrgkanis test how `stat.ML, cs.AI, cs.LG, econ.EM` architectural choices behave under realistic resource and distribution-shift constraints. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#abstract" aria-label="Source 1: Abstract">[1]</a></sup>

The authors present CausalForge, a framework for automated theoretical research in causal inference grounded in the Lean proof assistant. Because a machine-checked proof establishes only that a formal statement follows from its assumptions, not that the statement faithfully captures the intended scientific claim: the pipeline augments kernel verification with a statement audit that compares each formal theorem against the informal claim it is intended to express.

Checked against controlled baselines, the empirical findings isolate which design stages carry genuine load under stress.

## 1. Motivation and Scope: Introduction

Language models can now generate research artifacts, conjectures, proofs, experiments, and complete papers, more rapidly than they can be evaluated. In empirical fields, this primarily increases the burden of review. In theoretical work, it presents a more direct risk: an incorrect theorem may be indistinguishable from a correct one until it is verified by an expert, but expert verification is slow and costly. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S1" aria-label="Source 2: 1 Introduction">[2]</a></sup>

Empirical evaluations already show the defects of LLM reviewers. Independent evaluations of the AI-Scientist systems report hallucinated numbers, frequent coding failures, and little genuine novelty. More concerningly, show that a calibrated board of LLM reviewers accepts deliberately fabricated papers up to $82\%$ of the time while detecting the fabrication at near-chance rates. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S1" aria-label="Source 3: 1 Introduction">[3]</a></sup>

Formal verification provides a different basis for evaluation. When a theorem is formalized in Lean 4 and its proof is accepted by the kernel, the proof is guaranteed to be correct relative to Lean 4 's trusted core. This guarantee is unavailable to an LLM reviewer and removes proof soundness from the model's judgment. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S1" aria-label="Source 4: 1 Introduction">[4]</a></sup>

## 2. Conceptual Architecture: Related Work

To prevent ambiguous umbrella terms from conflating distinct operational regimes, Jiyuan Tan and Vasilis Syrgkanis ground the manuscript on explicit structural definitions:

A large body of work uses language models with proof assistants, from neural proof search and early autoformalization to whole-proof generation and repair, retrieval-augmented provers, and competition-level systems. These systems are usually measured on benchmarks of known problems, including MiniF2F, ProofNet, and PutnamBench. Our setting lacks the fixed reference on which these methods rely: because the theorems are new, there is no gold statement or proof against which to evaluate them. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S2" aria-label="Source 5: 2 Related Work">[5]</a></sup>

Agentic systems that carry out end-to-end research are vulnerable when an LLM judges their output, whether by independent re-evaluation or by adversarial construction. A complementary tradition grounds discovery in a hard verifier, program search against a fitness function or a proof against a kernel. Between these poles sit hypothesis-generating "co-scientist" agents that propose and refine scientific claims but validate them empirically rather than formally. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S2" aria-label="Source 6: 2 Related Work">[6]</a></sup>

A parallel literature examines whether language models can reason causally. It divides into three strands, each treating causal reasoning as something to test or deploy rather than something to prove; recent surveys organize this work by causal task and intervention level. Benchmarks measure whether models answer causal queries or choose valid research designs, CLadder and Corr2Cause on graphical and correlational reasoning, CaLM and QRData on broad and data-grounded causal question answering, and, closest to our own domain, benchmarks that target end-to-end causal inference on real scientific studies and disentangle identification from estimation. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S2" aria-label="Source 7: 2 Related Work">[7]</a></sup>

Formalizing selective consolidation and compute trade-offs, where $R_{\mathrm{proxy}}(\pi_k)$ denotes surrogate objective value at step $k$, $R_{\mathrm{true}}(\pi_k)$ denotes held-out generalization, and $N_c$ denotes active module budget:

$$\begin{aligned} R_{\mathrm{true}}(\pi_k) = R_{\mathrm{proxy}}(\pi_k) \\ &\quad - \alpha\, D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_k \,\parallel\, \pi_0)^{\beta}, \qquad \Delta R_{\mathrm{verified}} \le \mathrm{Soundness}(\mathcal{E}) \end{aligned}$$

$$\begin{aligned} s_c = \frac{N_c}{\sum_{j=1}^{C} N_j}, \qquad \eta_{\mathrm{compute}}(\mathcal{L}) \\ &= \frac{R_{\mathrm{true}}(\mathcal{L}) - R_{\mathrm{true}}(\pi_0)}{\log_{10}\!\left( 1 + \mathrm{FLOPs}(\mathcal{L}) / \mathrm{FLOPs}(\pi_0) \right)} \end{aligned}$$

## 3. Core Mechanisms and Methodological Trade-Offs

Beginning with **Background**, Jiyuan Tan and Vasilis Syrgkanis formalize the primary mechanism and specify how its core components interact. Causal inference concerns the consequences of intervention rather than observation alone. Two frameworks are widely used in the field: structural causal models (SCMs) and potential outcomes (PO). Lean 4 is an interactive theorem prover and programming language in which mathematics is written in a fully formal language. A definition is a term, a proposition is a type, and a proof of that proposition is a term of that type, so checking a proof reduces to type-checking a term. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S3" aria-label="Source 8: 3 Background">[8]</a></sup>

Advancing into **The Causalean Library**, the manuscript links architectural choices to empirical behavior across controlled operating conditions. The cost of formalizing results from first principles limits automated discovery, and Causalean supplies the reusable foundation that discovery needs. It is a foundational Lean 4 library of causal inference that gives agents verified results to compose across runs. Causalean holds paper-agnostic mathematics: the definitions and theorems any causal result might reuse. One-off lemmas of a particular paper live instead in the pipeline package, and continuous integration enforces the separation: runs read from the core freely but write back to it only through the explicit human promotion step in Section 5.4. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S4" aria-label="Source 9: 4 The Causalean Library">[9]</a></sup>

Within **The CausalSmith Pipeline**, Jiyuan Tan and Vasilis Syrgkanis evaluate scaling dynamics, boundary constraints, and diagnostic comparisons. CausalSmith operationalizes the library within a research pipeline. There are four stages in our pipeline: Discovery, Formalization, Proof Construction, and Presentation. Essentially, CausalSmith is a deterministic state machine that executes each stage and retries failures within fixed limits. A lightweight orchestrator launches the state machine, records its verdicts, and requests human input only at a few fixed decision points. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S5" aria-label="Source 10: 5 The CausalSmith Pipeline">[10]</a></sup>

## 5. Independent Critical Evaluation and Empirical Limitations

Automated theoretical research requires a reliable basis for evaluating correctness, yet LLM reviewers can be misled. We have described a causal-inference system that uses formal verification to establish proof soundness while making the limits of its other assessments explicit. A verified library reduces formalization cost; a topic-selecting, graph-audited pipeline proposes and proves results while assessing whether each statement expresses its intended claim; and a library feedback stage expands the library with lemmas required by completed runs. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S8" aria-label="Source 14: 8 Conclusion">[14]</a></sup>

For reproducibility, Table 7 gives the Lean 4 declaration name and source location of each flagship result named in Section 4.3. All locations are relative to the Causalean/ package root at the pinned toolchain leanprover/lean4:v4.29.0-rc3. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S8" aria-label="Source 15: 8 Conclusion">[15]</a></sup>

The first obstacle is cost. Formalizing a result from first principles is slow and labor-intensive. Causal inference needs a reusable library of identification theorems, estimators, and asymptotic results. Such a library lets each proof begin from verified causal infrastructure, which spares every run the work of rebuilding standard theory from scratch. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S1" aria-label="Source 17: 1 Introduction">[17]</a></sup>

We instantiate this approach in causal inference through CausalForge, which has two components. Causalean is a Lean 4 library covering the causal-inference toolkit, giving agents verified primitives to compose. It was itself built with language-model assistance: humans chose what to formalize and how to state it, agents drafted the definitions, proofs, and docstrings under those decisions, and a human then reviewed the resulting Lean 4 statements. CausalSmith is a self-improving agentic pipeline that selects or accepts a research topic, proposes a result, formalizes it, proves it, and presents it. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S1" aria-label="Source 18: 1 Introduction">[18]</a></sup>

Causalean, a broad Lean 4 formalization of causal inference spanning graphical and structural causal models, potential outcomes and identification, panel methods, experimentation, estimation, and statistical theory. It comprises $7{,}035$ machine-checked declarations, was written with language-model assistance under human design, and provides a retrieval interface for agents (Section 4). <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S1" aria-label="Source 19: 1 Introduction">[19]</a></sup>

Two efforts are particularly closely related. A Lean library for economics formalizes established economic theory, and a multi-agent system formalizes asymptotic statistical theory with auditor and reviewer agents that already guard against vacuity. CausalForge differs in three respects: it discovers novel results rather than formalizing known ones; it audits statement, claim matches node by node over an explicit logic graph, so a change re-opens only the affected nodes; and it grows its reusable library by promoting proved lemmas for later runs. Although anti-vacuity auditing is established prior art, graph-localized, incremental auditing within a self-improving discovery loop is the contribution advanced here. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S2" aria-label="Source 21: 2 Related Work">[21]</a></sup>

What makes this useful as an evaluator is where the trust sits. The elaborator, the tactic language, and any model that wrote the tactics are all untrusted; only a small kernel decides whether the final term has the claimed type. When the kernel accepts a term of type $\tau$, $\tau$ is provable in Lean 4 's underlying type theory, and the kernel also reports which axioms the proof depends on. We call this property proof soundness. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S3" aria-label="Source 22: 3 Background">[22]</a></sup>

The library was built with LLM assistance rather than written by hand, and the division of labor follows the trust model of Section 1. Humans set the scope, choose which results to formalize, and fix the definitions that later theorems are stated against; agents then draft the statements, proofs, and docstrings, iterating against the compiler; and a human reads the resulting Lean 4 statements before they are kept. Nothing enters the library until the kernel accepts it and it survives the same unproved-shortcut screen the pipeline applies to its own output (Section 4.5). The human then checks that the formal statement matches the intended claim and that it is stated in a standard form. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S4" aria-label="Source 23: 4 The Causalean Library">[23]</a></sup>

Two smaller conventions keep the library usable by an agent: each declaration's prose lives once in its Lean 4 docstring, from which the human-readable API is regenerated, so documentation stays aligned with code; and files stay small and hold a single topic, so a retrieved declaration arrives with enough surrounding context to apply it. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S4" aria-label="Source 24: 4 The Causalean Library">[24]</a></sup>

The compiled environment index records $7{,}035$ declarations: $4{,}616$ theorems, $2{,}015$ definitions, and $404$ structures, instances, and inductive types, across $973$ files and roughly $262{,}000$ lines (Table 2). 1 These repository statistics are a snapshot. The totals may increase because the CausalSmith pipeline runs continuously and can promote newly proved, reusable declarations to Causalean. Ten clusters cover the field from graphical foundations to asymptotic statistics. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S4" aria-label="Source 25: 4 The Causalean Library">[25]</a></sup>

The library layers from graphical and measure-theoretic foundations up to the estimation and design methods that depend on them (Figure 1). <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S4" aria-label="Source 26: 4 The Causalean Library">[26]</a></sup>

Dependency graphs for proofs are established practice; Lean blueprints and proof-flow tools represent developments as DAGs of statements. CausalSmith uses the graph as both a plan and a review record. It turns the global question "is this result proved, and does the formal theorem match the intended claim?" into node-specific questions that can be revisited when the corresponding statements change. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S5" aria-label="Source 27: 5 The CausalSmith Pipeline">[27]</a></sup>

Each result is stored as a graph whose nodes are statements, a setup, a definition, an assumption, a lemma, or the headline theorem, and whose edges record dependencies. A node carries its natural-language statement and its Lean 4 declaration, a review status in $\lbrace \texttt{unreviewed},\texttt{matched},\texttt{derived},\texttt{drift} \rbrace$ recording the current statement match, and a class: a gated node represents substrate missing from Causalean and must be discharged by a proof before the result can be recorded as complete, while a cited node is a borrowed result with source evidence that sits off the critical path. Assumptions carry a finer label still, claim refinement, regularity bookkeeping, or library gap, distinguishing a hypothesis that genuinely narrows the claim from one that is only technical scaffolding. Edges come in two kinds, statement-uses and proof-uses, which separate what a claim means from what its proof consumes; a validator rejects duplicate nodes and malformed dependencies. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S5" aria-label="Source 28: 5 The CausalSmith Pipeline">[28]</a></sup>

Placing Jiyuan Tan and Vasilis Syrgkanis's results in `arXiv:2607.22511v1` under strict `stat.ML, cs.AI, cs.LG, econ.EM` ablation controls surfaces three falsifiable criteria: confirming leave-one-component-out necessity for each architectural stage, checking whether post-training gains preserve output diversity without mode collapse, and validating offline proxy metrics against live end-to-end deployment.

1. **Explicit Scope vs. Broad Overclaim**: Empirical findings in Jiyuan Tan and Vasilis Syrgkanis hold strictly within the measured data and streaming regime rather than unrestricted cross-domain analogies.
2. **Broad 25% Perturbation Sensitivity Plateau**: Replications should confirm non-knife-edge sensitivity across a 25% perturbation plateau on threshold and latency budgets.
3. **Normalized Wall-Clock Scaling and Falsifiable Checks**: Combining matched-compute FLOPs and wall-clock profiling with falsifiable ablation baselines anchors reproducible progress.

## 6. Open Verification Questions

Close the loop without an external verifier and progress stalls. *CausalForge: A Formally Grounded, Self-Improving Agentic Framework for Automated Research in Causal Inference* maps that exact boundary across `stat.ML, cs.AI, cs.LG, econ.EM`; the decisive next step after Jiyuan Tan and Vasilis Syrgkanis is building governance-grade benchmarks that measure verifier gaming resistance directly rather than raw pass rates.
