## Abstract

In *CausalForge: A Formally Grounded, Self-Improving Agentic Framework for Automated Research in Causal Inference* (`arXiv:2607.22511v1`), Jiyuan Tan and Vasilis Syrgkanis test how `stat.ML, cs.AI, cs.LG, econ.EM` architectural choices behave given realistic resource and spectrum-shift constraints. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#abstract" aria-label="Source 1: Abstract">[1]</a></sup>

We present CausalForge, a framework for automated theoretical research in causal inference grounded in the Lean proof assistant. Because a machine-checked proof establishes only that a formal statement follows from its assumptions, not that the statement faithfully captures the intended scientific claim: the pipeline augments kernel verification with a statement audit that compares each formal theorem against the informal claim it is intended to express.

Checked against controlled baselines, the experimental findings isolate that design stages carry genuine load given stress.

## 1. Motivation and Scope: Introduction

Language models can now generate research artifacts, conjectures, proofs, experiments, and complete papers, more rapidly than they can be evaluated. In empirical fields, this primarily increases the burden of review. In theoretical work, it presents a more direct risk: an incorrect theorem may be indistinguishable from a correct one until it is verified by an expert, but expert verification is slow and costly. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S1" aria-label="Source 2: 1 Introduction">[2]</a></sup>

Empirical evaluations already show the defects of LLM reviewers. Independent evaluations of the AI-Scientist systems report hallucinated numbers, frequent coding failures, and little genuine novelty. More concerningly, show that a calibrated board of LLM reviewers accepts deliberately fabricated papers up to $82\%$ of the time while detecting the fabrication at near-chance rates. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S1" aria-label="Source 3: 1 Introduction">[3]</a></sup>

Formal verification provides a different basis for evaluation. When a theorem is formalized in Lean 4 and its proof is accepted by the kernel, the proof is guaranteed to be correct relative to Lean 4 's trusted core. This guarantee is unavailable to an LLM reviewer and removes proof soundness from the model's judgment. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S1" aria-label="Source 4: 1 Introduction">[4]</a></sup>

## 2. Conceptual Architecture: Related Work

Within laying the conceptual foundation, Jiyuan Tan and Vasilis Syrgkanis separate key operational primitives unambiguously:

Agentic systems that carry out end-to-end research are vulnerable when an LLM judges their output, whether by independent re-evaluation or by adversarial construction. A complementary tradition grounds discovery in a hard verifier, program search against a fitness function or a proof against a kernel. Between these poles sit hypothesis-generating "co-scientist" agents that propose and refine scientific claims but validate them empirically rather than formally. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S2" aria-label="Source 5: 2 Related Work">[5]</a></sup>

- **Two efforts are particularly closely related**: A Lean library for economics formalizes established economic theory, and a multi-agent system formalizes asymptotic statistical theory with auditor and reviewer agents that already guard against vacuity. CausalForge differs in three respects: it discovers novel results rather than formalizing known ones; it audits statement, claim matches node by node over an explicit logic graph, so a change re-opens only the affected nodes; and it grows its reusable library by promoting proved lemmas for later runs. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S2" aria-label="Source 6: 2 Related Work">[6]</a></sup>

## 3. Mechanistic Coupling and Theoretical Properties

Beginning alongside **Background**, Jiyuan Tan and Vasilis Syrgkanis formalize the primary process as well as specify how that core components interact. Causal inference concerns the consequences of intervention rather than observation alone. Two frameworks are widely used in the field: structural causal models (SCMs) and potential outcomes (PO). Lean 4 is an interactive theorem prover and programming language in which mathematics is written in a fully formal language. A definition is a term, a proposition is a type, and a proof of that proposition is a term of that type, so checking a proof reduces to type-checking a term. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S3" aria-label="Source 7: 3 Background">[7]</a></sup>

Advancing into **The Causalean Library**, the manuscript links architectural choices toward experimental behavior over controlled operating conditions. The cost of formalizing results from first principles limits automated discovery, and Causalean supplies the reusable foundation that discovery needs. It is a foundational Lean 4 library of causal inference that gives agents verified results to compose across runs. Causalean holds paper-agnostic mathematics: the definitions and theorems any causal result might reuse. One-off lemmas of a particular paper live instead in the pipeline package, and continuous integration enforces the separation: runs read from the core freely but write back to it only through the explicit human promotion step in Section 5.4. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S4" aria-label="Source 8: 4 The Causalean Library">[8]</a></sup>

Within **The CausalSmith Pipeline**, Jiyuan Tan and Vasilis Syrgkanis evaluate scaling kinetics, boundary constraints, and diagnostic comparisons. CausalSmith operationalizes the library within a research pipeline. There are four stages in our pipeline: Discovery, Formalization, Proof Construction, and Presentation. Essentially, CausalSmith is a deterministic state machine that executes each stage and retries failures within fixed limits. A lightweight orchestrator launches the state machine, records its verdicts, and requests human input only at a few fixed decision points. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S5" aria-label="Source 9: 5 The CausalSmith Pipeline">[9]</a></sup>

## 5. Critical Assessment of Assumptions and Scaling

Automated theoretical research requires a reliable basis for evaluating correctness, yet LLM reviewers can be misled. We have described a causal-inference system that uses formal verification to establish proof soundness while making the limits of its other assessments explicit. A verified library reduces formalization cost; a topic-selecting, graph-audited pipeline proposes and proves results while assessing whether each statement expresses its intended claim; and a library feedback stage expands the library with lemmas required by completed runs. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22511v1#S8" aria-label="Source 13: 8 Conclusion">[13]</a></sup>
