## Abstract

Studying *SkillProx: Self-Evolving Agent Skills via Proximal Textual Gradient Descent* (`arXiv:2608.07449v1`), Mingxuan Zheng, Yujin Zhou, et al. (7 authors) formulate `cs.AI, cs.CL` state transitions that allocate computation or consolidation capacity selectively under measurable uncertainty. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#abstract" aria-label="Source 1: Abstract">[1]</a></sup>

The authors introduce SkillProx, a proximal-gradient-inspired forward, backward framework that couples closed-loop diagnostic evolution with utility-aware proximal refinement. Component ablations demonstrate the complementary effects of closed-loop diagnosis and proximal refinement. 1 Code will be available at https://github.com/Steven011018/SkillProx.

Assessed independently, the key strength lies in linking selective compute or memory consolidation directly to testable efficiency-retention frontiers.

## 1. Motivation and Scope: Introduction

Large language model (LLM) agents have demonstrated strong capabilities in solving complex tasks through reasoning, tool use, and interaction with external environments. To reuse procedural knowledge acquired from previous experience, recent agents increasingly externalize task-solving procedures, tool-use strategies, and domain-specific heuristics as persistent and editable skills. Previous studies primarily focus on skill synthesis, constructing reusable skills from demonstrations, successful trajectories, or external documents. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#S1" aria-label="Source 2: 1 Introduction">[2]</a></sup>

Despite this progress, existing skill evolution methodologies face two critical limitations. (i) Unverified forward updates. An LLM-generated diagnosis is commonly treated as a valid update direction without verifying the realized effect of the resulting skill edit. Once a patch is generated, it is typically committed directly, and its effectiveness is neither measured through re-execution nor provided as feedback to subsequent diagnoses. (ii) Unregulated skill growth. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#S1" aria-label="Source 3: 1 Introduction">[3]</a></sup>

To explore this question, the authors conduct an analysis of open-loop, growth-oriented skill evolution, which points to two actionable design principles. On the forward side, the authors find that updates cannot be judged from their diagnostic text alone, but re-executing the updated skill on the same task batch directly reveals whether an update helps or hurts, suggesting that realized outcomes should gate whether an update is retained. On the backward side, the authors find that accumulated knowledge can be audited at the unit level, where removing negative-utility content improves accuracy from 46% to 54%, suggesting that skill health should be maintained through selective consolidation rather than continual growth. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#S1" aria-label="Source 4: 1 Introduction">[4]</a></sup>

Recent LLM agents increasingly externalize procedural knowledge into skills, structured bundles of instructions, scripts, and resources that are loaded into the agent's context at inference time and improve task execution without any weight update. ## 5. Critical Evaluation and Boundary Conditions

Across SpreadsheetBench Verified, WikiTableQuestions, and HiTab, SkillProx improves skill performance with multiple backbone LLMs under both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#S6" aria-label="Source 11: 6 Conclusion">[11]</a></sup>

The proposed method addresses two distinct failure modes in iterative skill evolution. The forward stage must determine whether a proposed update actually improves behavior, while the backward stage must remove redundant or harmful content that remains after repeated accumulation. These two problems require different forms of feedback. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#S6" aria-label="Source 12: 6 Conclusion">[12]</a></sup>
