## Abstract

Studying *SkillProx: Self-Evolving Agent Skills via Proximal Textual Gradient Descent* (`arXiv:2608.07449v1`), Mingxuan Zheng, Yujin Zhou, et al. (7 authors) formulate `cs.AI, cs.CL` state transitions that allocate computation or consolidation capacity selectively under measurable uncertainty. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#abstract" aria-label="Source 1: Abstract">[1]</a></sup>

The authors introduce SkillProx, a proximal-gradient-inspired forward, backward framework that couples closed-loop diagnostic evolution with utility-aware proximal refinement. Component ablations demonstrate the complementary effects of closed-loop diagnosis and proximal refinement. 1 Code will be available at https://github.com/Steven011018/SkillProx.

Assessed independently, the key strength lies in linking selective compute or memory consolidation directly to testable efficiency-retention frontiers.

## 1. Motivation and Scope: Introduction

Large language model (LLM) agents have demonstrated strong capabilities in solving complex tasks through reasoning, tool use, and interaction with external environments. To reuse procedural knowledge acquired from previous experience, recent agents increasingly externalize task-solving procedures, tool-use strategies, and domain-specific heuristics as persistent and editable skills. Previous studies primarily focus on skill synthesis, constructing reusable skills from demonstrations, successful trajectories, or external documents. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#S1" aria-label="Source 2: 1 Introduction">[2]</a></sup>

Despite this progress, existing skill evolution methodologies face two critical limitations. (i) Unverified forward updates. An LLM-generated diagnosis is commonly treated as a valid update direction without verifying the realized effect of the resulting skill edit. Once a patch is generated, it is typically committed directly, and its effectiveness is neither measured through re-execution nor provided as feedback to subsequent diagnoses. (ii) Unregulated skill growth. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#S1" aria-label="Source 3: 1 Introduction">[3]</a></sup>

To explore this question, the authors conduct an analysis of open-loop, growth-oriented skill evolution, which points to two actionable design principles. On the forward side, the authors find that updates cannot be judged from their diagnostic text alone, but re-executing the updated skill on the same task batch directly reveals whether an update helps or hurts, suggesting that realized outcomes should gate whether an update is retained. On the backward side, the authors find that accumulated knowledge can be audited at the unit level, where removing negative-utility content improves accuracy from 46% to 54%, suggesting that skill health should be maintained through selective consolidation rather than continual growth. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#S1" aria-label="Source 4: 1 Introduction">[4]</a></sup>

## 2. Conceptual Architecture: Related Works

Precision across `2608.07449v1` begins with Mingxuan Zheng, Yujin Zhou, et al. (7 authors)'s explicit partitioning of terms and state boundaries:

Recent LLM agents increasingly externalize procedural knowledge into skills, structured bundles of instructions, scripts, and resources that are loaded into the agent's context at inference time and improve task execution without any weight update. Because authoring such skills by hand is label-intensive, hard to scale, and prone to human, machine cognitive misalignment, a first line of work studies skill synthesis, which distills reusable skills from agent experience: Trace2Skill distills trajectories into skill artifacts, EvoSkill discovers and edits skills through iterative failure analysis with validation-based selection, CoEvoSkills couples a skill generator with a co-evolving surrogate verifier to build multi-file packages without ground-truth tests, and SkillComposer build skills via decomposing skill construction into three learnable operations. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#S2" aria-label="Source 5: 2 Related Works">[5]</a></sup>

However, SkillsBench shows that one-shot synthesis is far from sufficient: curated skills help unevenly across domains, and self-generated skills yield negligible or even negative gains. Motivated by this observation, a second line of work builds on prior advances in text-space optimization, notably TextGrad's backpropagation of natural-language feedback and GEPA's reflective, Pareto-guided prompt evolution, and transplants these ideas to the skill artifact itself. SkillOpt bounds each edit with a textual learning rate and a held-out gate, while SkillGrad treats the skill package as a structured parameter updated by trajectory-level loss evidence, textual gradients, and momentum. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#S2" aria-label="Source 6: 2 Related Works">[6]</a></sup>

## 5. Methodological Critique and Scope Guardrails

Across SpreadsheetBench Verified, WikiTableQuestions, and HiTab, SkillProx improves skill performance with multiple backbone LLMs under both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#S6" aria-label="Source 12: 6 Conclusion">[12]</a></sup>

The proposed method addresses two distinct failure modes in iterative skill evolution. The forward stage must determine whether a proposed update actually improves behavior, while the backward stage must remove redundant or harmful content that remains after repeated accumulation. These two problems require different forms of feedback. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#S6" aria-label="Source 13: 6 Conclusion">[13]</a></sup>

A forward, backward formulation of skill evolution. We formulate skill evolution as composite optimization over task performance and skill complexity, revealing two missing components in existing methods: outcome-grounded verification of forward updates and utility-guided regularization of accumulated knowledge. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#S1" aria-label="Source 15: 1 Introduction">[15]</a></sup>

The SkillProx framework. The authors introduce SkillProx, which combines closed-loop diagnostic co-evolution including same-batch re-execution, rollback, retry, and accept/reject memory with validation-gated proximal shrinkage. This design enables online prevention of harmful edits and retrospective removal of negative-utility knowledge. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#S1" aria-label="Source 16: 1 Introduction">[16]</a></sup>

Empirical validation of diagnostic, proximal co-evolution. Across three backbone LLMs, the authors compare SkillProx against six baselines on one in-distribution and two out-of-distribution benchmarks, observing performance gains in most evaluated settings. Component ablations further show that closed-loop diagnosis improves task accuracy, while proximal refinement provides additional gains through utility-aware knowledge consolidation. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#S1" aria-label="Source 17: 1 Introduction">[17]</a></sup>

Consolidating Accumulated Knowledge. Representative evolved skills exhibit repeated procedural instructions and task-specific solutions presented as reusable rules. Additional skill content therefore does not necessarily translate into additional task-solving capability. Leave-one-out evaluation further identifies accumulated knowledge units with negative utility, meaning that their removal improves validation performance. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07449v1#S3" aria-label="Source 18: 3 Preliminaries &amp; Motivation">[18]</a></sup>

Evaluating `arXiv:2608.07449v1` (*SkillProx: Self-Evolving Agent Skills via Proximal Textual Gradient Descent*) highlights three falsifiable checks on Mingxuan Zheng, Yujin Zhou, et al. (7 authors)'s framework: external verifier soundness sets the hard upper bound whenever learned proxies face optimization pressure, negative multi-round drift runs warrant disaggregated reporting, and matched test-time compute budgets must separate algorithmic gains from raw sampling volume.

Methodological review of `2608.07449v1` (*SkillProx: Self-Evolving Agent Skills via Proximal Textual Gradient Descent*) isolates three scope limits for `cs.AI, cs.CL`:

1. **Surrogate vs. Analogy Boundary (`1 Introduction`)**: Descent bounds in `2608.07449v1` hold strictly where local smoothness matches real trajectories, avoiding loose analogies.
2. **Non-Knife-Edge Sensitivity Plateau (`3 Preliminaries & Motivation`)**: Behavior reported by Mingxuan Zheng, Yujin Zhou, et al. (7 authors) must persist across a $\pm 25\%$ perturbation plateau in gating weights.
3. **Sparse Realization and Falsifiable Splits**: Scaling `cs.AI, cs.CL` operators requires $\mathcal{O}(\lvert E \rvert)$ message passing instead of cubic $\mathcal{O}(N^3)$ decompositions on falsifiable stress splits.

## 6. Open Verification Questions

Verifier quality sets the hard ceiling. *SkillProx: Self-Evolving Agent Skills via Proximal Textual Gradient Descent* separates regimes where external checks sustain genuine refinement from regimes where unanchored loops drift, leaving one concrete experimental question open: how can Mingxuan Zheng, Yujin Zhou, et al. (7 authors)'s evaluator hierarchy be stress-tested online before proxy overoptimization sets in?
