## 1. Failure Modes When Embodied Cognition Ignores Statistical Physics

Counterexamples over dual axes constrain any valid cross-domain law. Disembodied symbol tables detached from state-transition consequences confuse verbal fluency about an object with knowing what happens when weight, friction, or timing intervenes (Gibson, 1979). Living observers carve a continuous world into meaningful objects not as detached taxonomy exercises, but according to the concrete interactions their bodies can reliably execute (Gibson, 1979). Modeling Embodied Cognition without Statistical Physics leaves critical instability boundaries invisible in single-domain sweeps.

A flat stone affords stepping across a stream to a hiker, hiding beneath to a crab, and nothing climbable to an ant; meaning emerges at the interface between environment and effector (Lee, 1976). Plunging gannets fold their wings at a precise threshold of retinal expansion rate so they enter turbulent ocean water streamlined regardless of prey size or dive height (Gibson, 1979; Wilson, 1975). Robotic grasp synthesis and tactile active sensing converge faster when visual features predict contact wrench cones directly rather than reconstructing millimeter-dense background meshes (Wilson, 1975).

## 2. Joint Operator Structure: Optic-Flow Invariants and Time-to-Contact and Integrating Out Short-Wavelength Shells

James J. Gibson's ecological optics highlights invariant optic-flow ratios such as tau, time-to-contact, which specify actionable affordances directly without fragile depth reconstruction (Lee, 1976). In Embodied Cognition, where $\theta(t) = 2\arctan(R / z(t))$ denotes subtended retinal visual angle of an obstacle of unknown physical radius $R$ at unknown metric distance $z(t)$ (reducing to $\theta \approx 2R / z$ under the paraxial small-angle condition $R / z \ll 1$), and $\tau(t) = \sin\theta(t) / \dot{\theta}(t)$ denotes exact optical time-to-contact (Lee, 1976).

$$ \theta(t) = 2\arctan\!\left(\frac{R}{z(t)}\right) \underset{R/z \ll 1}{\approx} \frac{2R}{z(t)}, \qquad \tau(t) = \frac{\sin\theta(t)}{\dot{\theta}(t)} = \frac{z(t)}{-\dot{z}(t)} $$

$$ e^{-\mathcal{H}\prime[S\prime]} = \mathrm{Tr} _ {\lbrace s_i \rbrace}\, \mathcal{P}(S\prime, s)\, e^{-\mathcal{H}[s]} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad K\prime = R_b(K) $$
