## Abstract

This paper examines resource as a structured concept rather than a primitive. Taking its standard definition as a point of departure, it isolates the properties that give the concept its internal organization and argues that these properties, not the label, are what the concept's explanatory work depends on.

## 1. Introduction

The concept of resource is widely invoked yet unevenly specified. A working definition holds that resource is all the materials available in the environment which are technologically accessible, economically feasible and culturally sustainable and help to satisfy needs and wants. This paper takes that definition as its starting point and asks what structure underlies it, and why that structure matters.

## 3. Conclusion

This analysis has treated resource not as a self-evident given but as a structured concept whose properties can be stated and examined. The organization surveyed above suggests that its apparent simplicity conceals a definite internal structure, one that rewards further and more formal study.
