# The Shape of Empathy

Few ideas are invoked as often, and examined as rarely, as empathy. The usual shorthand, that empathy is generally described as the ability to perceive another person's perspective, to understand, feel, and possibly share and respond to their experience, is accurate enough, yet it leaves the more interesting question untouched: why does the concept hold together at all?

It is also worth noting how empathy connects to However. The relation is not incidental but part of what fixes the concept in place.

To call empathy well understood would be premature; to call it mysterious would be lazy. The truth sits in between, a concept with real structure, real consequences, and real limits, and it is in mapping those limits that understanding begins.
