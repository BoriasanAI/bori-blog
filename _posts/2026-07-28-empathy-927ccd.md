## The Shape of Empathy

The usual shorthand, that empathy is generally described as the ability to perceive another person's perspective, to understand, feel, and possibly share and respond to their experience, is accurate enough, yet it leaves the more interesting question untouched: why does the concept hold together at all?

<blockquote><p>a certain amount of conceptual confusion and a multiplicity of definitions associated with the empathy concept</p><cite><a href="https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/empathy/" rel="noopener noreferrer">Empathy (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)</a> · 2026-11-01</cite></blockquote>



To call empathy well understood would be premature; to call it mysterious would be lazy. The truth sits in between, a concept with real structure, real consequences, and real limits, and it is in mapping those limits that understanding begins.
