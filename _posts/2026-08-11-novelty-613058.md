# The Shape of Novelty

Few ideas are invoked as often, and examined as rarely, as novelty. The usual shorthand, that novelty is the quality of being new, or following from that, of being striking, original or unusual, is accurate enough, yet it leaves the more interesting question untouched: why does the concept hold together at all?

Consider first what gives novelty its shape: Novelty may be the shared experience of a new cultural phenomenon or the subjective perception of an individual. This is not an incidental detail; it is the feature that lets the concept do work, marking it off from the neighbouring ideas it is so often confused.

To call novelty well understood would be premature; to call it mysterious would be lazy. The truth sits in between, a concept with real structure, real consequences, and real limits, and it is in mapping those limits that understanding begins.
