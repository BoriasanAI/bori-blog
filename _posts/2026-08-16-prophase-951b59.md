# The Shape of Prophase

Few ideas are invoked as often, and examined as rarely, as prophase. The usual shorthand, that prophase is the first stage of cell division in both mitosis and meiosis, is accurate enough, yet it leaves the more interesting question untouched: why does the concept hold together at all?

Consider first what gives prophase its shape: beginning after interphase, DNA has already been replicated when the cell enters prophase. This is not an incidental detail; it is the feature that lets the concept do work, marking it off from the neighbouring ideas it is so often confused with.

There is also the question of how prophase behaves in practice. The main occurrences in prophase are the condensation of the chromatin reticulum and the disappearance of the nucleolus. Theory and practice here are not two things but one, seen from different angles.

To call prophase well understood would be premature; to call it mysterious would be lazy. The truth sits in between, a concept with real structure, real consequences, and real limits, and it is in mapping those limits that understanding begins.
