## Abstract

Examining *TamilEOT: A Dataset and Model for Semantic End-of-Turn Detection in Tamil Telephone Speech* (`arXiv:2609.05631v2`), Santhoshkumar V dissect an operational bottleneck in `cs.CL` pipelines through module-level ablations and compute-matched baselines. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05631v2#abstract" aria-label="Source 1: Abstract">[1]</a></sup>

Without a model of the language, systems fall back to a fixed silence timeout: too short, and the agent interrupts; too long, and every turn pays the full wait. We release TamilEOT, to our knowledge the first open dataset and detector for semantic end-of-turn detection in a South Indian language: 18,485 labelled turn boundaries from 116 real Tamil telephone conversations, two audio-only detectors built on Smart Turn v3's architecture, and a turn-level companion set of 4,774 whole turns with their pause structure.

Evaluated under matched-compute scrutiny, the study separates architectural lift from sampling inflation and pinpoints deployment guardrails.

## 1. Motivation and Scope: Introduction

At each pause, a voice agent must decide whether the speaker has finished or is still thinking. Voice activity detection cannot make that call: it detects sound, not intent. Systems built on VAD alone fall back to a fixed silence timeout, a single dial with two bad ends: too short, and the agent talks over a speaker who paused mid-sentence; too long, and every completed turn waits for a timer that exists only to catch the pauses. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05631v2#S1" aria-label="Source 2: 1 Introduction">[2]</a></sup>

A semantic end-of-turn (EOT) detector removes the dial. It listens to the last few seconds of the speaker's audio and predicts completion from prosody, without waiting for a transcript. Smart Turn v3 is the most widely deployed open model of this kind, and its released weights cover a fixed language list that does not include Tamil. This paper is about detectors that can be inspected, retrained and run locally, where a fixed language list is a hard limit rather than a feature request. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05631v2#S1" aria-label="Source 3: 1 Introduction">[3]</a></sup>

A dataset. 18,485 labelled turn boundaries from 116 real Tamil narrowband telephone conversations, split by call, released CC BY 4.0 (Section 3). To our knowledge it is the first open dataset for semantic end-of-turn detection in any South Indian language. From the same calls we also release 4,774 whole turns with their internal pause structure, for the turn-taking questions an 8-second window cannot express (Section 3.5). <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05631v2#S1" aria-label="Source 4: 1 Introduction">[4]</a></sup>

## 2. Conceptual Architecture: Related work

Precision across `2609.05631v2` begins with Santhoshkumar V's explicit partitioning of terms and state boundaries:

Voice Activity Projection learns turn-taking events self-supervised from the future voice activity of both speakers, and needs both channels. Smart Turn v3 takes the deployment-shaped version of the problem: one channel, the user's, and a binary verdict on the last 8 seconds. It is a Whisper-tiny encoder with a classification head, about 8M parameters, quantised to int8 and running in tens of milliseconds on CPU. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05631v2#S2" aria-label="Source 5: 2 Related work">[5]</a></sup>

The ETD dataset is, to our knowledge, the first public corpus aimed directly at end-turn detection, combining TTS-generated dialogue with real conversational audio. Smart Turn's own training corpus is largely TTS. Neither covers Tamil, and neither is narrowband telephone speech, which is the acoustic condition most Indic voice agents actually run in. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05631v2#S2" aria-label="Source 6: 2 Related work">[6]</a></sup>

The closest analogue to this paper is a recent study of Thai end-of-turn detection, which builds a Thai baseline from transcribed subtitles and classifies at token boundaries. That work is text-only and depends on a transcript; ours is audio-only and runs before one exists. The two approaches are complementary: a transcript-based detector cannot fire until ASR has emitted, and an audio-based one cannot read lexical cues. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05631v2#S2" aria-label="Source 7: 2 Related work">[7]</a></sup>

Expressing the bounded-resource optimization frontier, with $R_{\mathrm{proxy}}(\pi_k)$ denoting training proxy return, $R_{\mathrm{true}}(\pi_k)$ denoting out-of-sample fidelity, and $N_c$ denoting stage capacity:

$$\begin{aligned} R_{\mathrm{true}}(\pi_k) = R_{\mathrm{proxy}}(\pi_k) \\ &\quad - \alpha\, D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_k \,\parallel\, \pi_0)^{\beta}, \qquad \Delta R_{\mathrm{verified}} \le \mathrm{Soundness}(\mathcal{E}) \end{aligned}$$

$$\begin{aligned} s_c = \frac{N_c}{\sum_{j=1}^{C} N_j}, \qquad \eta_{\mathrm{compute}}(\mathcal{L}) \\ &= \frac{R_{\mathrm{true}}(\mathcal{L}) - R_{\mathrm{true}}(\pi_0)}{\log_{10}\!\left( 1 + \mathrm{FLOPs}(\mathcal{L}) / \mathrm{FLOPs}(\pi_0) \right)} \end{aligned}$$

## 3. Governing Operators and Design Trade-Offs

In examining **Dataset**, Santhoshkumar V trace how outer-loop interventions trade episodic flexibility versus long-term persistence. The audio is the `ta_IN_*_{Left,Right}` family of SPRING-INX Tamil R1, released CC BY 4.0 by SPRING Lab, IIT Madras. It is 116 two-party Tamil telephone conversations, and it has one property that decided the whole project: each conversation ships as one audio file per speaker, each leg transcribed separately by a human. Which file the audio came from is the speaker label. There is no diarization step and therefore no speaker-error rate propagating into the labels. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05631v2#S3" aria-label="Source 8: 3 Dataset">[8]</a></sup>

Shifting out of inference-time loops to **Labels**, this review analyzes how self-generated rationales as well as process-level supervision consolidate onto durable policy updates. The first label set was rule-derived: a floor change at a segment end is complete; a pause wholly inside one transcript segment is incomplete. This is the standard construction, and it is cheap enough that it is rarely questioned. We ran a blind listening pass: 197 clips stratified by (provenance, label), no label shown, no metadata, audio only, one listen each. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05631v2#S4" aria-label="Source 9: 4 Labels">[9]</a></sup>

At this epistemic center of the framework, **Model and training** establishes why every recursive improvement claim stands strictly bounded by each soundness of its evaluator. The architecture is unchanged from Smart Turn v3: a Whisper encoder over an 8-second log-mel window, attention pooling, and a binary classification head. We train the whole stack, with no layers frozen. We replicate Smart Turn v3's model class and initialise the encoder exactly as its own training script does, from openai/whisper-tiny or openai/whisper-base, with the pooling and classification heads randomly initialised. We do not fine-tune from the released v3 checkpoint. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05631v2#S5" aria-label="Source 10: 5 Model and training">[10]</a></sup>

## 5. Methodological Critique and Scope Guardrails

Code is BSD-2-Clause. The dataset derives from SPRING-INX Tamil R1 (CC BY 4.0, SPRING Lab, IIT Madras) and carries the same licence; we do not own the recordings and redistribute them under attribution. The models are BSD-2-Clause, matching pipecat-ai/smart-turn, whose architecture and training code they reuse; their encoder weights are initialised from openai/whisper-tiny and openai/whisper-base. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05631v2#Sx1" aria-label="Source 14: Availability">[14]</a></sup>

Two pipeline steps (the labeller bake-off and the label-quality analysis) reproduce their reports byte-identically from a bare clone with no corpus, no GPU and no cloud account, using the text-stripped label tables committed to the repository. <sup class="review-citation"><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05631v2#Sx1" aria-label="Source 15: Availability">[15]</a></sup>

## 6. Open Verification Questions

One question stays unsettled. While *TamilEOT: A Dataset and Model for Semantic End-of-Turn Detection in Tamil Telephone Speech* connects mechanism design in `cs.CL` directly to measurable failure boundaries, testing that inductive invariants survive multi-generation covariate shift beyond Santhoshkumar V's current scope necessitates matched-FLOPs prequential ablations.
