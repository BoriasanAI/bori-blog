## The Shape of Planning

It is tempting to treat planning as self-evident, yet the moment one looks closely the idea resists a simple answer. At its core, planning is the process of thinking regarding the activities required to achieve a desired goal. That is a starting point, not a conclusion, and the distance between the two is what these paragraphs try to trace.

What complicates the picture is this: planning is based on foresight, the fundamental capacity for mental time travel. The idea is less tidy than its textbook form suggests, and that untidiness is precisely where its interest lies.

None of this settles the question of planning for good. What it suggests is that the concept is sturdier than its casual use implies, and that beneath the shorthand lies a structure worth taking seriously. The ideas we treat as obvious are usually the ones most worth re-examining.
