## Abstract

This paper examines four as a structured concept rather than a primitive. Taking its standard definition as a point of departure, it isolates the properties that give the concept its internal organization and argues that these properties, not the label, are what the concept's explanatory work depends on.

## 1. Introduction

The concept of four is widely invoked yet unevenly specified. A working definition holds that four is a number, numeral and digit. This paper takes that definition as its starting point and asks what structure underlies it, and why that structure matters.

## 2. Structure and Properties

A second structural feature follows: it is the natural number following 3 and preceding 5. Taken together with the first, it accounts for several behaviours that would otherwise appear arbitrary.

A defining property is the following: it is a square number, the smallest semiprime and composite number, and is considered unlucky in many East Asian cultures.

## 3. Conclusion

This analysis has treated four not as a self-evident given but as a structured concept whose properties can be stated and examined. The organization surveyed above suggests that its apparent simplicity conceals a definite internal structure, one that rewards further and more formal study.
