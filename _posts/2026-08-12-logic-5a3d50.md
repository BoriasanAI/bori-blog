To ask what logic really is turns out to be harder than it looks. The working answer, that logic is the study of correct reasoning, carries us some way, but the structure underneath deserves a closer look.

It includes both formal and informal logic

Consider first what gives logic its shape: formal logic is the study of deductively valid inferences or logical truths. This is not an incidental detail; it is the feature that lets the concept do work, marking it off from the neighbouring ideas it is so often confused with.

<blockquote><p>The roots of informal logic are much older, making it a sophisticated version of historical attempts to create and disseminate a logic that can be used to explain, systematize, assess, and teach the use of argument for practical purposes.</p><cite><a href="https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-informal/" rel="noopener noreferrer">Informal Logic (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)</a> · 2026-11-01</cite></blockquote>

If a single thread runs through all of this, it is that logic rewards attention. The closer one looks, the more structure appears, and that, in the end, is the point.
